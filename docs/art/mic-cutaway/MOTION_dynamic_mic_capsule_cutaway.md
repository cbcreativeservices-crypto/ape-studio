Companion to `dynamic_mic_capsule_cutaway.svg` (rest state, transparent, `viewBox="0 0 480 360"`) and

## 0. What this asset teaches (and the simplifications it declares)

The dynamic microphone is the loudspeaker run backwards — a **generator**: sound pressure `p` moves the diaphragm, the
voice coil moves in the radial field of the annular gap, and an EMF `e = B·l·v` is induced — proportional to coil
**velocity**, not displacement. A well-damped dynamic capsule is designed to be **resistance-controlled** (Wente & Thuras
1931; Olson 1947; Beranek 1954), so velocity follows pressure and the output waveform follows the pressure waveform;
displacement lags by 90°. That is the phase model drawn here:

| quantity | formula | note |
|---|---|---|
| `p(t)` (at the diaphragm) | `A·sin φ` | plane wave arriving from +x; compression = positive |
| `v(t)` (inward, −x) | `∝ p` | resistance-controlled |
| `dx_screen(t)` | `+X·cos φ` | outward positive on screen; inward displacement lags p by 90° |
| `e(t)` (output, lug A = +) | `A·sin φ` | Shure polarity convention: positive pressure → positive voltage on pin 2 |
| `X` | **2.5** default, max **3** | **exaggerated ≈10³×** — real diaphragm excursion is sub-µm to µm |
| `T` | 1.2 s default | period; φ = 2π·t/T |

Declared simplifications: (0) the induced-current overlay assumes a resistive load is connected (current ∝ e); open-circuit there is EMF but no current; (i) pressure-gradient (cardioid) operation is *shown structurally* (rear ports, felt,
phase-shift passages, two cavities) with a static rear-path overlay, not modelled dynamically; (ii) a cardioid dynamic is
mass-controlled above ~100 Hz (SBE handbook), which would put x in anti-phase — the resistance-controlled case is drawn
because it states the design intent and makes e ∝ v legible; (iii) gap, coil wire and clearances are stretched for
legibility (real gap ≈ 0.5 mm, clearance ≈ 0.15 mm, wire ≈ 30 µm CCAW); (iv) the transformer, if any, lives in the handle
and is not drawn; (v) the pneumatic shock mount is omitted.
