## House style — derived from your approved term images

All prompts below already contain this; repeated here as the reference:

> Photorealistic product photograph, seamless pure-white background, soft even
> studio lighting with a subtle contact shadow beneath the object, three-quarter
> view angled diagonally across a square frame, object filling ~85% of frame,
> crisp focus, realistic materials, **no text, no logos, no branding**, generic
> unbranded product, 1240×1240.

**Delivery:** WebP, filenames exactly as given below (kebab-case, matching the
existing bucket convention), uploaded to the same public `glossary-images`
bucket. Send me the list when they're up and I map them to the connector cards.

**Accuracy note:** contact counts and locking features listed under "must show"
are verified facts from the lab's fact-checked records — an image that gets one
wrong teaches the wrong thing. Everything else can follow real product
references.

# PART 1 — XLR (highest priority: the lab's most-taught connector)

### 1. XLR male cable connector — `xlr-male.webp`
**Used by:** Lesson 3 card, Lesson 4 comparisons, Lesson 8 scenarios.
**Must show:** exactly **3 round pins**; nickel/silver pins in a black
cylindrical barrel; the latch groove along the shell; cable exiting the boot.

> Photorealistic product photograph of a generic 3-pin XLR male cable
> connector, seamless pure-white background, soft studio lighting with a subtle
> contact shadow. Three-quarter view angled diagonally across a square frame,
> the pin end toward the lower-left and the black rubber cable boot with a short
> length of black cable exiting toward the upper-right. Matte-black metal barrel
> with a knurled grip ring, a black insulator face at the mating end carrying
> exactly THREE silver round pins in the standard XLR triangular arrangement,
> and the shallow latch groove visible along the shell. Crisp focus, realistic
> nickel and matte-black plastic materials, unbranded, no text or numbers,
> 1240×1240.

### 2. XLR female cable connector — `xlr-female.webp`
**Used by:** Lesson 3 card (mating/gender teaching), Lesson 4, Lesson 8.
**Must show:** exactly **3 socket holes** (not pins); the latch release tab on
the shell. This is NOT a mirrored male — female face geometry differs.

> Photorealistic product photograph of a generic 3-pin XLR female cable
> connector, seamless pure-white background, soft studio lighting with a subtle
> contact shadow. Three-quarter view angled diagonally across a square frame,
> the socket end toward the lower-left and a black rubber boot with a short
> length of black cable exiting toward the upper-right. Matte-black metal
> barrel with a knurled grip ring; at the mating end a black insulator face with
> exactly THREE round socket holes with silver contacts visible inside, and the
> spring latch release tab visible on the side of the shell. Crisp focus,
> realistic materials, unbranded, no text or numbers, 1240×1240.

### 3. XLR cable, both ends — `xlr-cable.webp` *(optional but valuable)*
**Used by:** Lesson 1 (what travels), Lesson 4 (mic cable vs look-alikes).

> Photorealistic product photograph of a complete generic XLR microphone
> cable, seamless pure-white background, soft studio lighting with subtle
> contact shadows. Both ends shown: a 3-pin XLR male connector and a 3-pin XLR
> female connector, with black flexible cable looped loosely between them
> across a square frame. Matte-black barrels, silver pins on the male end,
