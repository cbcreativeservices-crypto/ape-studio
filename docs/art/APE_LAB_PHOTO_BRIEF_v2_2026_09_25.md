## 1 · The look — one rule, then numbers

**Owner's ruling (2026-09-21): "app theme is dark, learning is white background."**

The app's cards are black. The photograph sits in a light tile 300 px wide on a phone
(`02_EXAMPLES/plate_1_worked_example_mic.png`). A white, bright, one-subject photograph reads
at that size; a dark room does not. So:

| | Object shots (Stage 5 sort, extension cord, XLR) | Finding shots (Final Inspection) |
|---|---|---|
| Background | seamless white, evenly lit, no floor line | the real place the finding names, **bright and tidy**, evenly lit, no dust, no gloom |
| Subject | ONE object, filling 75–85 % of the frame | ONE fault, large and centred, filling most of the frame |
| Camera | three-quarter view, slightly above, sharp front to back | eye level or slightly above, the fault sharp |
| Light | soft, from the upper left, faint contact shadow | soft, even, daylight-neutral |
| Cables | round black audio cable (6 mm), blue category cable (6 mm), thick black snake (20 mm), orange/black rubber power cord (10 mm). **Never thin coloured single wires** — that reads as electrical building wire and has been rejected twice | same |
| Text | none — no lettering, labels, arrows, numbers, logos | none, **except** finding 15 (§4), which is about two labels |
| People | none, no hands | none |
| Size | **1200 × 896 px exactly**, PNG (the frame is 1200 : 896; any other size is cropped — your d04 at 1200 × 655 lost its sides) | **1200 × 896 exactly** |

The 22 photographs the owner approved are in `03_APPROVED_STYLE_REFERENCE/`. They are the
standard: white, big, sharp, cut back to show construction, nothing else in frame. Match them.

## 2 · Where the images land (three cards)

**Stage 2 — the workbench card** (`plate_1`). Tap a cable type; the card opens with the photo
above the words. 13 of 14 done. One left: the extension cord (§4, group 2).

**Stage 5 — THE SORT** (`plate_2`). The learner sees a neutral grey **line drawing** of a piece
of hardware and answers *"Would you hang cable on this?"* — APPROVE or REJECT. **After the
answer** the verdict block opens and your photograph lands at the top of it under the caption
**THE REAL THING — APPROVED CABLE HARDWARE** or **THE REAL THING — NEVER A CABLE SUPPORT**,
then the green or red verdict text. Because the drawing asked the question, the photograph must
show the **same object**, unmistakably, and — for approved hardware — show it **doing its job**
(anchored, carrying cable, with the feature that makes it approved visible). For the six
"never" items, show the thing itself, bare, nothing hanging on it.

**Stage 13 — Final Inspection** (`plate_3`). The learner taps a numbered marker on a drawing of
the facility. The card opens with the **finding label** (a sentence naming the cable, the place
and the fault), then your photograph under **AS FOUND ON THE WALK**, then CLASSIFY (eight
categories) and CORRECT. The photograph sits directly under those words, so it must show
**exactly what the words say**: the cable named, the place named, the fault named. This is why
a generic "excessive bend" in a ceiling (your d04) cannot sit under "Mic line folded hard
behind the stage box".

Nothing else in the lab has a photo slot today. The Stage 6 rack issues and the Stage 8 ceiling
walk are drawings only — **do not make images for them**.
