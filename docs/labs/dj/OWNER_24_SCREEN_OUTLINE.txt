I suggest 24 lesson screens, progressing from basic setup to advanced operation. Each screen should keep its related explanations, equipment, controls, demonstrations, and practice together.

Start learners with a simple virtual two-deck system. Introduce additional equipment and routing as they progress.

Order	Lesson screen	Keep these topics together on that screen
1	Welcome and safe operation	What the lab teaches; safe listening; headphone precautions; playback volume; stop and reset controls.
2	Meet the DJ equipment	Turntables, media players, controllers, mixers, interfaces, headphones, and speakers. Compare vinyl, laptop, standalone, and modular systems.
3	Connections and signal types	Cable identification; inputs versus outputs; stereo versus balanced mono; mic, phono, and line levels; audio versus USB, MIDI, and network data.
4	Build your first DJ system	Connect two sources, mixer, headphones, and speakers; follow the complete signal path; power connections; startup and shutdown.
5	Load and play music	Track selection, loading, play/pause, transport cue, jog controls, active-deck identification, playlists, and basic media preparation.
6	Set gain and read meters	Input selection, trim, channel meters, master meters, headroom, clipping, and matching tracks with different recorded levels.
7	Headphone cue and booth monitoring	Private preview, cue selection, cue/master blend, split cue, headphone level, and independent booth level.
8	Channel faders and crossfaders	Channel levels, crossfader assignments, bypass, response curves, smooth blends, and introductory cuts.
9	EQ isolators and filters	Frequency bands, boosts and cuts, isolator behavior, high-pass and low-pass filters, resonance, and managing overlapping bass.
10	Tempo beatmatching and phrasing	BPM, beats, bars, phrases, tempo adjustment, jog nudges, beat offset, and gradual tempo drift.
11	Beatgrids sync and quantize	Track analysis, first-beat placement, incorrect grids, half/double BPM, synchronization, and quantized actions.
12	Cue points loops and samples	Transport cue versus headphone cue, hot cues, loop creation, loop length, loop exit, beat jump, and sample playback.
13	Effects and their routing	Delay, echo, reverb, selected modulation effects, dry/wet balance, feedback, beat timing, and channel versus master effects.
14	Playback speed pitch and key	How speed affects pitch, key lock, time stretching, key shifting, processing artifacts, and musical transition choices.
15	Stems and source separation	Vocal, drum, bass, and instrumental isolation; stem mixing; original stems versus separated audio; leakage and artifacts.
16	Laptop software and audio interfaces	Controller connections, device selection, drivers, channel assignments, sample rate, buffer size, latency, and dropouts.
17	Vinyl turntables and phono audio	Cartridge and stylus, tonearm, tracking force, anti-skate, slipmats, phono stages, RIAA equalization, grounding, and isolation.
18	Digital vinyl systems	Control vinyl, control-signal routing, compatible interfaces, calibration, playback modes, tracking problems, and recovery.
19	Microphones announcements and talkover	Microphone connections, receiver output levels, gain, mute, ducking, feedback prevention, and microphone inclusion in different output feeds.
20	PA speakers subs and venue connections	Powered/passive systems, audience versus booth sound, stereo/mono feeds, summing, crossovers, bass management, placement, and venue-engineer handoff.
21	Recording and streaming	Recording feeds, capture levels, microphone inclusion, software routing, monitoring loops, playback verification, and service-specific restrictions.
22	Hybrid DJ systems	Drum machines, synthesizers, samplers, extra controllers, audio routing, MIDI control, clock, and synchronization.
23	Performer changeovers and backup playback	Incoming/outgoing systems, computer and media handovers, level matching, independent backups, equipment failure, and maintaining playback.
24	Troubleshooting and complete system challenge	Diagnose silence, distortion, hum, missing channels, cue problems, recording faults, and playback failures; finish with a complete event setup and recovery exercise.

How each lesson screen should work

Keep the equipment diagram visible while the learner moves through:

1. Understand — a short explanation.
2. Explore — identify connections and operate controls.
3. Listen or observe — hear the result and see the relevant meters.
4. Practice — complete a guided task.
5. Check yourself — solve a short independent challenge.

Definitions and supporting explanations should open inline or in an overlay, preserving the learner’s equipment settings and position.

Screens 16–18 can form optional equipment paths after the core lessons. A standalone-system learner should be able to continue to microphones and PA systems without first completing laptop configuration and DVS.

Include a small troubleshooting exercise within each relevant lesson; reserve Screen 24 for problems that combine several parts of the system.
