# LAB 2 IDIOPHONES — source-lesson survey (I01–I12)

Surveyed 2026-10-04. Read in full: 23 Lab 2 files plus Headed-Tambourine (Lab 1, M08) for I04 only. Bracketed numbers [n] are each lesson's own reference numbers. All lessons cite URLs ("Read source <url>"; Gong gives bare URLs). No reference without a URL was found.

Shorthand used below:
- "Shure ≥30 cm" = Shure *Home Recording Guide for Beginners*: a general percussion starting point of "at least 30 cm". It is cited in 9 lessons as the anchor for their own trial ranges.
- "Shure 45/60/135" = Shure *Microphone Techniques for Live Sound Reinforcement* pp. 30–33. Two mics aimed downward about 1½ ft (45 cm) above the instrument, either about 2 ft (60 cm) apart or with grilles together at about 135° (coincident).
- "TRIAL" = a number the lesson itself labels as a reasoned teaching or audition range, not a published value.
- "9-part" = the master plan's required structure: 1 Source/task · 2 Mic selection · 3 Core positions · 4 Alternatives · 5 Studio/live · 6 Multi-mic · 7 Troubleshooting + safe setup · 8 Teaching exercise (repeatable comparison + observation sheet + criteria) · 9 Evidence audit.

### I01a — Hi-Hat Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Pro Audio Training Academy · Idiophones Lab · Research checkpoint 4 · October 3 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Top and bottom cymbals, "often dissimilar".
- Clutch, pedal, hi-hat rod, stand hardware.
- Open, partly open and closed positions. Top cymbal's rocking motion.
- Closure air stream (escaping between the cymbals at the closing edge).
- Stick/brush strike arc; strike area "roughly 2–3 cm from the edge" (DPA [1]; describes playing, not mic placement).
- Edge vs centre/bell zones of the top cymbal.
- Snare and snare rim, neighbouring cymbals, overheads.
- Player hand and forearm.
- Geometry UNSPECIFIED: diameter, cymbal gap, pedal travel, clutch height.

**MICROPHONES**
- Shure "pencil condenser" (generic) [3].
- Sennheiser e 914 (has a high-pass filter) [6].
- LEWITT LCT 040 MATCH [7].
- Audix SCX1HC, hypercardioid; the lesson calls Audix's preference "marketing" [10].
- DPA 4099, under-hat mounting option [1].
- Polar notes: cardioid rear null vs super/hypercardioid side-rear nulls with a rear lobe [1, 5, 10].

**POSITIONS**
- Shure: "within four inches of the hi-hat, away from the air puff at closure" [2].
- Shure: pencil condenser "roughly 10–15 cm away, pointing down near the far edge away from the snare" [3].
- DPA: "5–10 cm above the top cymbal", angled to reduce snare pickup [1].
- Sennheiser e 914: "A few centimetres above the outer edge, aimed downward", away from the closing-edge air current [6].
- Underside: DPA 4099 option; no number given [1].
- Rule: "Do not position the capsule in the plane of the opening between the two cymbals."

**SAFETY**
- Drummer stops before any mic is moved.
- Full-motion check: stick/brush path, rocking, open/closed travel, hand/forearm, snare rim, rod, clutch, pedal.
- Never clamp to a moving cymbal. Do not change the player's hat height.
- "no cited source gives one universally safe clearance".
- NIOSH: 85 dBA as an 8-hour TWA, with duration halved per 3 dBA [9].
- Mute outputs (including monitor and aux paths) before phantom changes; Yamaha ZG01 example [8].
- A pad helps only if the overloaded stage is downstream of it.

**MULTI-MIC**
- Mono check with hat + overheads + snare.
- Polarity is "not an automatic requirement"; it reverses electrical polarity, not acoustic delay [5, 12].
- "Do not automatically time-align every channel or rely on a fixed 3:1 distance rule."
- Opposing snare-mic strategies: aim the snare mic toward the hat (Shure [4]) vs point its rear rejection at the hat (LEWITT [11]).

**PROPOSED APP**
- Top and side views: hat pair, stick arc, cymbal travel, closure air path, snare, overheads, stand.
- Begin with the overhead contribution.
- Choices: no dedicated mic / shared snare / top mic / under-hat.
- Moving the mic changes written qualitative observations (attack, body, overtones, air pops, spill, phase, physical risk). "no fabricated numeric response curves".
- Unsafe placements "visibly unavailable".
- Live vs studio offered as text decisions.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- 9-part: mic selection is a paragraph, not a section. The teaching exercise is an "assessment scenario" with no observation sheet and no repeatable two-position comparison.
- Three distinct Shure/DPA/Sennheiser numbers (≈10 cm, 10–15 cm, 5–10 cm, "a few cm") are correctly presented as non-equivalent.
- Overlap with Lab 1 snare and overheads.
- No contradictions found. The expert content looks sound.

### I01b — Ride Cymbal Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Research checkpoint 6 · October 3 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Bell; bow / middle "ride area"; outer "crash area".
- Stand with sleeve, felts and wing nut (cymbal must move freely) [2].
- Floor tom immediately below; adjacent cymbals; overhead pair.
- Stroke arcs (stick tip vs shoulder); full swing after a crash; choke grip.
- Geometry UNSPECIFIED: size, weight and profile ("Size, weight, taper, bell, profile, lathing" are named only as variables).

**MICROPHONES**
- Sennheiser e 914 on ride (Blake Shelton tour) [7].
- DPA 4015, "wide-cardioid", tucked underneath cymbals (Broadway *& Juliet*) [8].
- Unnamed small condenser (Shure touring interview) [6].
- Audix DPQUAD 4-mic package: right overhead covers ride + floor tom [3].

**POSITIONS**
- Shure: condenser "about 1–2 ft above" the ride to cover remaining cymbals; described as broad coverage, not a spot [5].
- No spot distance or angle is given: "The sources do not supply a universal spot distance or exact angle."

**SAFETY**
- Stop the player. Map the largest swing, bell/bow arcs, choke, floor-tom path.
- No heavy boom over the performer without secure load support.
- "No source establishes a universally safe clearance in centimeters."
- NIOSH 85 dBA / 3 dBA [10]; Yamaha phantom [11].

**MULTI-MIC**
- Mono first, then stereo/PA.
- Polarity is conditional [9]. High-pass by context.
- A floor-tom mic under the ride may already contain the ride.
- Avoid automatic hard panning.

**PROPOSED APP**
- Top and side view labelled bell, bow/ride area, outer crash area, stand, floor tom, adjacent cymbals, stroke arcs, swing.
- Ask first whether the overheads suffice.
- Choices: wider overhead / safe top spot / advanced underside.
- Qualitative text changes as aim and distance move. No numeric spectral curves.
- Block unsafe positions. Live/studio selector. Link to the Drum Overheads lesson.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- Same 9-part gaps as Hi-Hat (no observation sheet; mic selection embedded).
- Checkpoint number 6 sits beside Hi-Hat's 4, with Crash/Splash/China unnumbered, so the numbering is inconsistent.
- Content is sound.

### I01c — Crash Cymbal Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Crash cymbal · October 3 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Outer crash area, bow, bell.
- Stand sleeve, felts, wing nut.
- Neighbouring snare, high tom, another crash, ride, vocal mic.
- Sticks, hands, choke; maximum swing and rebound ("changeable full-motion envelope").
- Models range "thin and light to heavy and thick".
- Geometry UNSPECIFIED: diameter.

**MICROPHONES**
- No model named.
- "A condenser is common". "Compact directional microphones" under cymbals (DPA, Killers tour) [6].
- Ribbon cautions (mounting, phantom, air) [8].
- Audix DPQUAD (overheads only) [5].

**POSITIONS**
- No numbers. Top-side spot is described as "a conditional teaching starting point, not a source-established distance".
- Underhead is upward-facing (DPA [4]).

**SAFETY**
- Full strike/choke/swing/rebound map.
- Under-cymbal mic needs extra care: "the plate can flex downward or rock toward it".
- No universal gap. NIOSH [7]. Phantom muting [8].

**MULTI-MIC**
- The crash reaches overheads, spot, tom and vocal mics at different times.
- Mono, then stereo/PA. Polarity is not a delay fix.
- No fixed polarity, no automatic sample alignment.

**PROPOSED APP**
- Top and side view: crash, bow, bell, neighbouring tom, sticks, hands, stand, changeable full-motion envelope.
- Begin with "hear the existing overheads".
- Compare wider overhead / top-side spot / advanced underhead, with qualitative labels.
- Block positions that intersect strokes or cymbal travel. Studio/live selector for feeds and wedges.
- Links to Drum Overheads, Ride and Drum Kit.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- No numeric starting point at all; the app would need an owner-approved TRIAL value or a numberless representation.
- No observation sheet.
- Heavy overlap with Ride/Splash/China; about 70% of the text is shared structure.

### I01d — Splash Cymbal Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Splash cymbal · October 3 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Small cymbal.
- Mounting variants: own stand; accessory arm (Meinl MC-CY2 [7]); inverted above another cymbal ("piggyback", Zildjian Rafael Barata [6]); FX Stack [6].
- Sleeve and felts.
- Lower-cymbal travel in a stack.
- The assessment uses an "eight-inch splash" on an accessory arm between a rack tom and a crash, plus a second splash inverted above a ride (scenario values).

**MICROPHONES:** none named. "A compact directional condenser may be practical."

**POSITIONS**
- No numbers. The lesson says the cited guides do not supply a splash-only recipe.
- The top-side spot is "an engineering starting suggestion".

**SAFETY**
- Map stick, choke, splash swing and the lower cymbal's movement.
- Do not loosen a deliberately tensioned stack.
- No universal clearance. NIOSH [9]. Phantom [10]. Ribbon compatibility.

**MULTI-MIC:** mono, then stereo/PA. Polarity is a conditional test [12]. High-pass is no isolation.

**PROPOSED APP**
- Selectable standalone / piggyback / stack, each with real hardware and its own motion envelope.
- First question: do the overheads carry the cue?
- Compare overhead / top / under-cymbal with qualitative effects.
- Block intersecting positions. Studio/live selector.
- Links to Overheads, Crash, Ride, Kit.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- No numbers; same structural gaps (no observation sheet).
- Stack and piggyback illustration is the hardest art in I01.
- Overlap with Crash.

### I01e — China Cymbal Miking Technique
**Header line:** "China cymbal · October 3 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Upturned edge/lip, shoulder, bow, cup.
- Specialty inverted cup (A Ultra Hammered [6]); holes, rivets, jingles (Sabian Paragon Diamondback [6]).
- Upright vs inverted mounting. Stack variant.
- Stand. Floor tom nearby.
- Sizes: "14-inch K Mini China" [2]. The 18-in A China High and 20-in Paragon appear only in reference URLs; general size is otherwise UNSPECIFIED.

**MICROPHONES**
- Shure Beta 181: side-address underhead form factor [11].
- "suitable small condenser" (generic).
- Know end- vs side-address before aiming.

**POSITIONS:** no numbers. "no cited source specifies a universal China angle or distance."

**SAFETY**
- The lip has a different trajectory when inverted.
- An underhead mic may clear the stick arc and still sit in the downward cymbal travel.
- No universal distance. NIOSH [9]. Phantom [10].

**MULTI-MIC:** mono at working balance. Polarity is conditional [12]. Monitor sends per zone.

**PROPOSED APP**
- Side view of upright and inverted mounts with lip, shoulder, bow, cup, stand, sticks, swing.
- Learner chooses accent / ride pattern / both, then decides whether the overheads work.
- Optional top/underhead positions with qualitative effects. Block intersections. Studio/live selector.
- Links to Overheads, Ride, Crash, Splash, full kit.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- Strong point: Jeff Hamilton's upright ride use [3] counters the "always inverted" myth.
- No numbers. No observation sheet.

### I02 — Cajon Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Cajon · October 3 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Box body; frontplate (striking surface).
- Playing zones: centre = bass "thump"; top corners = slap.
- Internal snare: fixed wires (JC50BW [3]) or adjustable strings (Buleria [5]); some models have no snares.
- Sound port: rear (JC50BW) or front-facing (Slaptop AEVLPS [4]). Internal bass-reflex structures (Buleria [5]). Factory port pop filter [8].
- Seated player straddling the top: both hand arcs, knees, ankles, heel accessories, rocking/lifting, exit path.
- Accessories: Meinl MPMCC padded port clamp [12]; Schlagwerk CMH10 adapter plate [13]; BP40 base plate with internal condenser [14].
- Geometry UNSPECIFIED: box dimensions, port diameter and position height.

**MICROPHONES**
- Shure KSM137, cardioid condenser, front, in a two-mic demo [6].
- Generic "low-frequency dynamic instrument mic" (Audix FP7 package [10]).
- Cardioid rear rejection (LEWITT [11]).

**POSITIONS**
- Shure: front KSM137 "about 6–7 inches away at a slight angle"; second mic "just inside a rear hole" [6].
- Paul White (SOS): front cardioid "below the top edge, angled toward the center, at roughly 30–40 cm"; rear mic angled "about 20 cm away" [7].
- Meinl: mic "at the front-facing port" on Slaptop [4].

**SAFETY**
- Keep the mic outside both hand arcs, knees, ankles, heel accessories, rocking motion.
- "Never position a rigid mic where the player could land on it."
- No clamp to thin wood or into a port unless designed for it. No loose mic inside the box near the snare wires.
- No universal clearance. NIOSH 85 dBA / 3 dBA [15].

**MULTI-MIC**
- Front + port: solo each, then the mono sum.
- White recommends reversing one channel's polarity in his front/rear setup. The lesson teaches comparing both polarities instead [7, 9, 16].

**PROPOSED APP**
- Front and side views of a rear-port snare cajón plus a front-port variant.
- Mark strike zones, internal wires, port airflow, hand paths, legs, seating, exit routes.
- Choose single front / port / wider, then optionally add a second mic with a mono-sum comparison and a polarity switch.
- Qualitative bass, slap, air-pop risk, spill, feedback. No numeric spectrum.
- Block positions in a hand arc or exit path, and incompatible internal clamps.
- Studio/live selector. Link to hand percussion and ensemble labs.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- Refs [5] and [8] are the identical URL (Meinl AEBLMY).
- The Shure figure is in inches only and the White figures in cm only; no conversion is given (6–7 in ≈ 15–18 cm).
- The teaching exercise is an assessment scenario: no observation sheet and no repeatable distance comparison.
- The idiophone classification is argued correctly.
- Overlap with Lab 5 percussion ensembles.

### I03a — Handheld Shaker Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Standard handheld shaker · October 3, 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Enclosed shell with internal fill; the lesson implies a cylinder ("cylinder's end, side").
- Grip. Playing arc (marked "movement zone").
- Meinl Crystal Shaker Studio vs Live versions [1].
- Geometry UNSPECIFIED: shell size and shape.

**MICROPHONES**
- No model named. SDC, LDC and dynamic all allowed.
- Cardioid default; omni in a quiet room.
- Cardioid strongest rear rejection; super/hyper rear lobes [4].
- DPA Jonas Brothers tour: dedicated shaker/tambourine mic, model not named here [5].

**POSITIONS**
- TRIAL "roughly 30–60 cm (1–2 ft) from the center of the playing arc".
- Shure "at least 30 cm" [3].
- Exercise: "approximately 30 cm, then … around 60 cm".
- Distance is measured "to the player's usual center, not to the closest point at one instant".

**SAFETY**
- Stand and cable outside the path, clear of face and hands.
- Never solve feedback by raising gain.
- Phantom never to a wireless output. Secure boom against being pulled down.

**MULTI-MIC**
- A single mono spot is usually enough.
- Room mic only if needed, with a timing/mono check.
- Spot + main: mono check. "Don't automatically apply a fixed distance rule."

**PROPOSED APP:** ABSENT.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS**
- No Proposed-app section.
- Teaching exercise has a pass criterion but no observation sheet.
- No dedicated safety section; safety is spread inline plus "Safety and scope".
- Studio/live, multi-mic and troubleshooting are present.

**PROPOSED APP:** ABSENT.

**MULTI-MIC:** spot + main mono; no mandatory polarity switch.

**PROPOSED APP:** ABSENT.

**AUDIO:** none.

**QUALITY FLAGS:** no Proposed-app section; no observation sheet. Otherwise sound.

- Support: in hand or on foam with space underneath; not flat on carpet.

- Geometry UNSPECIFIED: dimensions.

**MICROPHONES**
- No model named. Cardioid condenser or dynamic.

- TRIAL "around 25–50 cm (10–20 in.) from the block".

- Exercise: "around 30 cm and one around 50 cm".

**SAFETY:** clear of mallet path including rebound and a missed stroke; no split block; NIOSH [9].

**MULTI-MIC:** one mic per set first; mono check when several mics hear the same blocks.

**PROPOSED APP:** ABSENT.

**AUDIO:** none.

### I05d — Güiro Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Güiro · October 3, 2026"

**ANATOMY TO DRAW**
- Hollow ridged tube (gourd, wood, fiberglass, metal per Grinnell [2]); ridges / textured playing surfaces (several on the fiberglass model [6]).

### I06a — Triangle Miking Technique
**Header line:** "Triangle · October 3, 2026"

### (d) Disagreements between (and within) lessons

1. **Shure ≥30 cm baseline vs closer numbers.**
   - Cowbell TRIAL starts at 20 cm; Woodblock at 25 cm.
   - Shure's own tambourine example is 6–12 in (15–30 cm) (M08).
   - Shure's glockenspiel example is 10–15 cm.
   - These Shure sources disagree with the "at least 30 cm" generic rule.
2. **One-mic overhead TRIAL heights:** Vibraphone 45–75 cm and Xylophone 45–75 cm vs Marimba 60–100 cm vs Glockenspiel 30–60 cm. Defensible by size, but unexplained across lessons.
3. **Coincident vs near-coincident.** Marimba and Xylophone exercises say "near-coincident"; their text and Shure say coincident (grilles together, 135°). Vibraphone's exercise says coincident.
4. **Polarity.** Cajón cites White's routine front/rear flip; every other lesson treats polarity as a conditional diagnostic only (the Cajón lesson itself also rejects the routine flip).
5. **Hi-hat snare-mic strategy.** Shure (aim the snare mic at the hat for shared pickup) vs LEWITT (point the cardioid rear at the hat). Both appear in one lesson as goal-dependent.
6. **Hi-hat close distances:** Shure "within four inches" / Shure "10–15 cm" / DPA "5–10 cm" / Sennheiser "a few centimetres".
7. **Cajón:** front 6–7 in (Shure) vs 30–40 cm (White); port "just inside" vs ~20 cm.
8. **Mallet safety.**
   - Marimba: Yamaha soft mallets and a damage warning vs PAS hard/unconventional mallets.
   - Xylophone: Yamaha "resin" suitable vs a Yamaha educator warning on plastic.
9. **Glockenspiel bar material:** Yamaha general article says aluminium alloy; YG-2500 and Adams say steel.
10. **Triangle:** Grover prefers handheld; PAS allows mounting.
11. **China:** "inverted and crashed" convention vs Jeff Hamilton's upright ride use.
12. **Gong:** Son Vo says a figure-eight ribbon avoids phase issues; the lesson rejects this.
13. **"Vibrato" label.** Rhodes Vari-Pan is a stereo pan (MK8/Suitcase); Wurlitzer "vibrato" is a mono amplitude tremolo. Consistent, but the labels collide.
14. **NIOSH framing varies.**
    - Cymbals and Cajón: 85 dBA 8-h TWA, 3 dBA exchange.
    - Cowbell: 85 dBA as an "assessment trigger".
    - Others: the "Turn It Down" bulletin without numbers.
    - Gong: none.
15. **Section templates.** The cymbal/Cajón template ("Critical source audit" + "Proposed app presentation" + assessment scenario) differs from the hand-percussion/mallet/EP template ("Evidence audit and limits" + numbered teaching exercise + pass criterion). Gong uses a third template.
