## 2. PER LESSON

### E01 Lead Vocal
- **Header:** "Pro Audio Training Academy Ensembles and Voice Lab Studio and Live Sound October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:** One singer (low male, high female, mixed, falsetto, contralto; delivery from intimate to belted to rapped). Studio: sturdy stand, shock mount, pop filter (angled, not parallel to the capsule), closed-back headphones, loudspeakers off. Live: handheld, headset/earset, wedge (cardioid: "directly in front of the singer"; hyper/super: "slightly to one side"), backline. Singer-with-instrument variant cross-referenced. UNSPECIFIED: stand height, pop-filter distance, monitor angle in degrees, room dimensions, headset capsule position.
- **MICROPHONES:** Neumann **KMS 104**: "stage condenser with a tight cardioid pattern, high SPL capability, built-in pop protection and a low-cut characteristic" [5], given as an example only. Generic types: large-diaphragm condenser, dynamic (Shure [1] for rock/metal/rap), ribbon/figure-eight [1], omni [4], cardioid/supercardioid/hypercardioid [4], headset/earset [3].
- **POSITIONS:** "20–30 cm (8–12 in) from the mouth" Neumann [2]; "10–20 cm (4–8 in)" Shure [1]; stage vocals "within 10 cm", peaks "above 135 dB in the direct field" DPA [3]; "approximately 10 dBFS of headroom above the highest peaks" (24-bit) [2]. Table: "On axis, 10–20 cm"; "On axis, 20–30 cm"; plus off-axis, above-angled-down and close-omni rows with no numbers.
- **SAFETY:** Stable stands, secure cables, feet clear. Never place headphones on a live mic. Never pop-test by blowing. Verify phantom power, cable and input before a live condenser is used. Stop the session if the singer reports pain, ringing or hearing change.
- **MULTI-MIC:** Minimal. Mono check for singer with instrument. Monitor at the actual null, with feedback troubleshooting. No 3:1.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** No observation sheet (it has an exercise and pass criterion only). The body says "within 10 cm" but the audit says "about 10 cm". "10 dBFS of headroom" is loose units: headroom is in dB, and the peak sits at −10 dBFS. The monitor wording ("in front of the singer") differs from E07 ("directly behind the microphone"): it is the same place, but an illustration must reconcile the two. Large overlap with E03 (same refs, same 135 dB and 10 dBFS figures) and E07.

### E02 Background and Harmony Vocals
- **Header:** "... Studio and Live Sound October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:** Trio or quartet. Three modes: one mic per singer, one shared mic, area/choir mics. Bluegrass group around one mic, with the loudest singer backing away and few or no monitors [2]. Choir mics "slightly above the singers and aiming toward the center row" [3]. Omni circle (singers circling an omni) [5]. "Two cardioids positioned back to back" [5]. Floor marks; wedges; IEMs; headset/earset. UNSPECIFIED: shared-mic distance and height, circle radius, back-to-back pair height, monitor angles.
- **MICROPHONES:** No models. Types: large-diaphragm condenser (shared), cardioid/supercardioid/hypercardioid [3,4], omni [5], condenser area mics [3].
- **POSITIONS:** Live handheld backing "about 1.5–3 inches (4–8 cm)" Shure [1]. 3:1: "if a microphone is 1 unit from its nearest singer, a neighboring microphone should be at least 3 units from that singer" [3]. DPA: "a distance factor of 3 or more ... a larger factor may be required with multiple neighboring directional microphones" [4].
- **SAFETY:** Stable stands and secure cables, sightlines, clearance for instruments and folders. Reduce channel level before moving a live mic. Comfortable monitor and headphone exposure.
- **MULTI-MIC:** Comb filtering when two mics hear one singer; mono sum; 3:1; do not feed choir mics into the choir monitor [3]; mute unused channels; mute/unmute comparison of natural vs reinforced level.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** Its **3:1 definition (distance from the singer to the neighbouring mic) differs from E04, E07 and E08 (mic-to-mic distance)**. Ref [6] duplicates ref [1] (same URL). No observation sheet. Heavy overlap with E04 and E05.

### E03 Rap and Rhythmic Vocal
- **Header:** "... October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:** Rapper with handheld or fixed stand mic, shock isolation, pop filter, headphones. Head, hand and stance movement inside a defined "working zone". Live: handheld or headset (capsule "near the mouth corner according to the manufacturer's instructions"), wireless pack, wedges. Optional vocal effect, talkbox-like device or monitor speaker. UNSPECIFIED: working-zone size, monitor angles, stand height.
- **MICROPHONES:** Shure **SM4** (named only as the source document "SM4 User Guide"; the body says "for some microphones") [2]. Types: dynamic (Shure [1]), condenser, high-SPL condenser, omni, cardioid/supercardioid/hypercardioid, headset/earset.
- **POSITIONS:** "1–6 inches" Shure [2]; "8–12 inches" condenser, Neumann [3]; table "Close dynamic, 2–6 in" (no source) and "8–12 in condenser"; DPA loose vocal "around 12 inches" [5]; stage "within 10 cm", peaks "can exceed 135 dB SPL" [6]; "approximately 10 dBFS of digital headroom" [3]. Troubleshooting: "Aim between nose and mouth" (no source).
- **SAFETY:** Cables clear of feet; reduce levels before moving a mic; hearing protection; stop on pain or ringing; do not make the performer shout over unsafe monitor levels; protect the capsule from moisture.
- **MULTI-MIC:** Doubled takes: mono-sum for comb filtering. Direct console signal plus stage mic: check polarity, delay and feedback.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** The table's "2–6 in" disagrees with the text's "1–6 inches". The SM4 (a studio condenser) guide is used as a general vocal-distance source. No observation sheet. About 60% overlaps E01.

### E04 Duets and Small Vocal Groups

Live: two directional mics, headsets, "one or two overhead/stand condensers" for a seated ensemble, a cappella quartet, backing group beside a band. UNSPECIFIED: all array dimensions (ORTF/XY geometry not given), semicircle radius, pair height, monitor positions.
- **MICROPHONES:** **AKG C414 XLS/XLII** (owner's manual §4.6.2 cited for shared, separate-hypercardioid, stereo and overdub options) [4], audit only. Types: cardioid, omni, large- and small-diaphragm condenser, figure-eight, hyper/supercardioid, condenser choir/hanging.

- **SAFETY:** Stable stands, secure cables, clear movement areas. Do not pass a live mic. Hearing protection during high-SPL checks.

- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.

- **QUALITY FLAGS:** **The body has no bracket citations at all**; the 6 references are listed but never tied to claims. **Exercise 4 raises monitors "Stop at the first sign of ringing"**, which deliberately approaches feedback; E10–E16 forbid provoking feedback, and the plan says to audit feedback demonstrations. ### E05 Choirs, Choruses, A Cappella
- **Header:** "... October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:** Choir on risers or tiers, in sections (SATB), semicircle or shallow arc for small groups, rows for large groups. Conductor visible. Area mics "slightly above mouth level", aimed at the vocal area or "toward the back row" for deep groups. Hanging mics. Stereo main pair at the audience-side acoustic center. Section spots "farther from the singers than a solo vocal microphone and above mouth level". Beatbox/vocal-percussion or soloist spot. Wedges. Exercise: chorus "in two rows". UNSPECIFIED: riser heights, pair height and distance, spot distances, hanging height.
- **MICROPHONES:** **AKG C414** manual [4], audit only. Types: directional condenser area mics; XY, MS, ORTF and A-B pairs; cardioid/super/hyper.
- **POSITIONS:** "roughly one microphone for each 6–9 feet of lateral section width", attributed to Shure (no #). 3:1: "if a microphone is about 2 feet from its nearest singer, the next microphone should be about 6 feet away" (no source). "From several feet away" (vague).
- **SAFETY:** Hanging-mic rigging, strain relief, clearance and vibration isolation must be approved by the responsible technician; secure installation only. Stop the ensemble before moving a mic. Hearing protection during high-SPL checks. Protected cable paths.
- **MULTI-MIC:** 3:1; mono/stereo; mute-check; "A closer spot can dominate localization even when it is lower in level"; polarity and delay are not cure-alls; no choir mics into choir monitors; fewest open mics.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** No bracket citations in the body. No observation sheet. "Headphones can misrepresent room width" is a reasonable but unsourced claim. Overlaps E06 almost line for line (same tables and troubleshooting).

### E06 Children's Voices and Choirs
- **Header:** "... October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:** Children's choir (exercise: "two rows"), risers, children with "a smaller vertical range than adults". Area mics aimed at mouths. Stereo pair. Individual stand mics. Handheld (only when the child can hold it safely). Headset and lavalier (lav "often less consistent for singing"). Transmitters, sandbags, cable ramps, hanging mics, supervising adults/teachers, emergency paths, costumes, scenery, water. UNSPECIFIED: mic heights for child stature, pair dimensions, headset position.
- **MICROPHONES:** AKG manual [4] (C414 per reference title). Types: directional condenser area, XY/MS/ORTF/A-B, handheld, headset, lavalier.
- **POSITIONS:** 3:1 "about 2 feet ... about 6 feet away"; "roughly one microphone per 6–9 feet of lateral section width" (Shure, no #); "slightly above mouth level". No dB values.
- **SAFETY / SAFEGUARDING (most extensive in the lab):**
  - A responsible adult or qualified crew controls setup, access, wireless coordination and placement changes.
  - Supervision, age-appropriate communication, and the venue's "child-safety and working-with-minors procedures".
  - Reduce sound at the source first. CDC [5] and NIDCD [6] are cited. OSHA [7] and NIOSH are described as "not child-specific performance targets".
  - "Measure sound at the children's ear locations". Short, quiet rehearsals. Low-volume monitors.
  - Stop the group before moving equipment. Sandbags, approved rigging, cable ramps. Keep water, costumes and scenery away from connectors and power.
  - Nothing may restrict breathing, movement or safe exit. Headset fitting supervised by authorized adults, cables and transmitters clear of the neck, and an "immediate way to mute or remove".
  - "Never hang a microphone over children's heads without a competent rigger and venue approval."
- **MULTI-MIC:** 3:1; mono; mute-check; no choir mics into choir monitors; spot localization.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** No bracket citations in the body. NIOSH is named but missing from the references. The "measure sound at ear locations" instruction gives no metric, weighting or target. No safeguarding policy source (the only sources are hearing-health). No observation sheet. Near-duplicate of E05.

### E07 Singer With Guitar or Piano
- **Header:** "... October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:**
  - Singer-guitarist: one mic, or two mics with the vocal mic's rejection aimed at the guitar and the guitar mic's rejection aimed at the mouth. Figure-eight side-null option. Pickup/DI.
  - Grand piano: lid open, short-stick or closed. Pair over the treble and bass strings, XY/MS/ORTF pair, or a single mic aimed at the middle strings. Boom for a singer at the keyboard.
  - Upright piano: front, above the open top, or rear.
  - Keyboard with amp or powered speaker. Wedge: "For cardioid vocal microphones, a monitor is generally placed directly behind the microphone". Side-fill, IEM.
  - UNSPECIFIED: boom geometry, lid stick height, monitor angles.
- **MICROPHONES:** **Shure SM4** guide (acoustic-guitar range) [2]; **AKG C314** manual (piano) [5]. Types: omni, directional, figure-eight, compact instrument mic, boundary/miniature.
- **POSITIONS:** Vocal "approximately 4–8 inches" (Shure, no #). Guitar "6–12 inches ... near the soundhole or twelfth fret" (SM4 guide, no #). Aim "toward the bridge or around the 12th fret". Piano "roughly 8–16 inches above the strings, with one microphone toward treble and one toward bass" (AKG, no #). 3:1: "If the vocal mic is about 1 foot from the mouth, try to keep the guitar microphone about 3 feet away from the vocal microphone".
- **SAFETY:** No stand where the pianist can strike it. Pedal, bench and emergency path clear. Open piano panels only with owner or venue approval, and secure mics so they cannot fall into the action. Do not damage the lid. Stop the performer before moving mics or changing connections. Leave fader travel in reserve.
- **MULTI-MIC:** 3:1; polarity and timing of pickup plus mic in mono; a figure-eight null "is not a guarantee of isolation"; check stereo and mono.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** **The 3:1 example (vocal mic 1 ft from the mouth, guitar mic 3 ft from the vocal mic) is physically unrealistic for a seated singer-guitarist and contradicts the 4–8 in vocal distance in the paragraph before it.** The lesson does admit the spacing may be impossible. No bracket citations in the body. No observation sheet. Overlaps E01, E08 and the Lab 4 guitar/piano lessons.

### E11 String Quartets and Larger String Sections
- **Header:** "... October 4, 2026".
- **ANATOMY / STAGING:**
  - Quartet (2 violins, viola, cello) seated as the players prefer, with eye contact and conductor visibility.
  - Main pickup "in front of and above the group".
  - XY: "Two cardioids with capsules nearly coincident; 90 degrees is a trial angle".
  - ORTF: "Two cardioids 17 cm apart at 110 degrees between axes".
  - AB: spaced, commonly omnis.
  - M/S: forward Mid plus sideways figure-eight Side.
  - Section mics for larger sections including double basses.
  - Decca Tree and outriggers are mentioned and deferred to E13.
  - Clip-on mounts; wedges; cello endpin.
  - UNSPECIFIED: section-mic heights, soloist spot distance.
- **MICROPHONES:** **SCHOEPS MSTC 74** (ORTF geometry source) [5]. Unnamed Neumann models in the 1998 Kronos report [9]; unnamed DPA in the 2015 Natalie Merchant report [10]. Types: cardioid, omni, figure-eight, miniature with a "specified wired adapter".
- **POSITIONS:**
  - Quartet "approximately 1–2 m in front and 1.8–2.5 m above the floor", labelled "instructor-selected trial ... not a published optimum".
  - ORTF "17 cm ... approximately 6.7 inches, not 6 inches" [5].
  - AB "40–60 cm" (DPA orchestra) [6].
  - Section mics "around 1–1.5 m from players and coverage of roughly three or four musicians" [7].
  - Delay "A 1 m path difference is about 2.9 ms at 343 m/s".
  - M/S "Left = Mid + Side and Right = Mid − Side".
- **SAFETY:** Instrument-specific mount with player approval. No ordinary adhesive on varnish; the cable must not pull on the instrument. Check bow sweep, left-hand travel, chair and endpin. Do not attach hardware to the bridge. Do not provoke feedback.
- **MULTI-MIC:** Do not apply 3:1 to a stereo array. Polarity cannot remove frequency-dependent delay. DPA warns against blind distance-derived delay [7].
- **APP PRESENTATION:** NONE.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included"; "do not include audio files in the lesson deliverables".
- **QUALITY FLAGS:** Says full orchestral arrays "belong in the Mixed Acoustic and Classical Ensembles lesson", which is **stale now that E14 exists**. No formal observation sheet (exercise 1 asks for a sketch and documentation). Well cited.

### (d) Disagreements between lessons
1. **3:1 definition.** E02: neighbouring mic ≥3 units from *that singer*. E04, E07, E08: next mic ≥3× from *that microphone*. E05, E06 use a 2 ft / 6 ft example, E04, E07, E08 use 1 ft / 3 ft. E16 calls it an isolation heuristic only. E11, E12 and E13 say it does not apply to stereo arrays.
2. **Feedback in exercises.** E04 Ex.4 raises monitors to "the first sign of ringing", and E07 says to "ring out only to a safe operating margin". E10, E12–E16 say do not provoke or sustain feedback.
3. **Monitor position wording.** E01, E02 and E04 say the cardioid wedge goes "directly in front of the singer". E07 says "directly behind the microphone". This is the same place; one canonical diagram is needed.
4. **Location of orchestral arrays.** E11 sends them to E13. E13 claims to be the last Lab 5 item. E14 exists but lacks the Tree and outrigger geometry that only E13 supplies.
5. **Live vocal distance.** E01 body "within 10 cm" vs its audit "about 10 cm". E02 backing "1.5–3 inches (4–8 cm)". E03 text "1–6 inches" vs table "2–6 in". E07 "4–8 inches".
6. **Brass close distance.** E10 "1–2 feet" (Shure) vs E16 "30–50 cm" (DPA) vs "2–5 ft" (Royer solo). E16 reconciles them; E10 does not mention the others.
7. **Main array height (different contexts, not errors, but an engine needs per-scene defaults).** E11 quartet 1.8–2.5 m (trial); E12 2–2.5 m (trial); E13 chamber 2–2.5 m (trial); E13 and E14 orchestra AB 3–4 m (DPA).
8. **ORTF spacing.** E11, E12 and E13 explicitly correct Shure's "six inches" to 17 cm. E04, E05, E06, E08, E14 and E15 name ORTF with no dimension.
