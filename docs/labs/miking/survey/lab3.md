# Lab 3 Aerophones — source survey (A01–A12)

Surveyed 2026-10-04. Read in full: the master plan and all 20 Lab 3 lesson text files. Source numbers [n] refer to each lesson's own reference list.

## Global facts (apply to every lesson below unless noted)

- **AUDIO:** All 20 lessons say "No audio examples are included." None mentions recordings, audio files or sound clips. The master plan also says "No audio examples" (lines 15, 277, 385).
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** **None of the 20 Lab 3 lessons has an app-presentation section**, and none says anything about radiation-pattern visuals for the app. A `# Curriculum and app presentation` section exists only in 7 Lab 1 docs (Bongos, Concert Bass Drum, Concert Snare, Djembe, Headed Tambourine, Timbales, Timpani). The nearest Lab 3 content is the "Tone/approach trials" table in each lesson (Musical aim → Starting trial → Listen for) and its "Teaching exercise".
  - **Cautions about radiation that bear on visuals (stated in text):**
    - Woodwinds and saxes radiate from both the tone holes and the bell, and the radiating region "changes with fingering/register". DPA calls it "complex" and recommends experimenting.
    - Trumpet notes say the bell's high-frequency output is "strongly directional".
    - Several lessons warn the instrument is "not a point source" (Tuba) and is "not simply a bell source" (Clarinet).
    - The pipe organ is a "distributed source".
    - Any radiation graphic would therefore need register-dependence and a "not to scale / qualitative" caution. The lessons themselves provide no polar data.
- **HYGIENE:** No lesson mentions hygiene, condensation, saliva or mic sanitising. This matters most for shared, mouth-proximate harp mics and for windscreens. Tuba is the only lesson that touches water: it mentions "water-key access" as a clearance item.
- **Hearing:** Every brass lesson cites NIOSH, as do Piccolo, Harmonica (via "player's hearing level") and Pipe Organ.
- **9-part structure gaps common to all 20 lessons:**
  - (2) **Microphone selection** never has its own section. It is scattered through the text, and most lessons deliberately refuse to name stand-mic models.
  - (8) **"Student observation sheet"** is absent everywhere. Each exercise gives 4 steps plus a "Pass criterion" but no sheet or field list.
  - (6) **Multi-mic** content is reduced everywhere to: hear each mic alone, then the mono sum, and do not auto-apply polarity or delay. No lesson mentions 3:1. Only the Pipe Organ lesson covers stereo-pair methods.
- **Date/checkpoint lines:** Format is "Pro Audio Training Academy · Aerophones Lab · <X> · <date>". For woodwinds, Piccolo, Harmonica, Accordion and Organ, <X> is "Studio and Live Sound" rather than the instrument name.
  - Dates conflict with the stated sequence. Bass trombone is dated Oct 3 but says it comes "after tenor", while Tenor trombone is dated Oct 4. Baritone sax is dated Oct 3; soprano, alto and tenor sax are dated Oct 4. Bassoon is Oct 4; oboe is Oct 3.

### A01a Trumpet — "Trumpet Miking Technique" — Aerophones Lab, Trumpet, October 3, 2026

- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Mouthpiece, with buzzing lips exciting the air column [3].
  - Bell, with a "strongly directional high-frequency field" [1].
  - Valves and the valve hand; tuning slides.
  - Mutes (each changes protrusion and hand use): straight, cup, Harmon/wah, plunger. Yamaha pickup mute (internal mic) [6].
  - Clip and gooseneck on the bell [5].
  - Posture: player behind the bell, bell moving and turning toward the rhythm section or conductor.
  - UNSPECIFIED: bell diameter, tubing wrap, valve type (piston implied), seated vs standing, bell height.
- **MICROPHONES:**
  - No stand-mic model is named.
  - Dynamic, condenser or ribbon "may be auditioned".
  - Cardioid vs supercardioid rear behaviour [4].
  - DPA 4099 on a brass clip, gooseneck aimed "between bell center and edge" [5].
  - Shure horn clamps (unnamed) [1].
  - Yamaha SILENT Brass pickup mute: a separate signal path, not external miking [6].
- **POSITIONS:**
  - "30–50 cm (about 1–1⅔ ft) from the bell, slightly off axis" — DPA [2].
  - "1–2 ft (about 30–60 cm)" — Shure [1].
  - Studio distant: "several-meter trial in a sufficiently large room" [2].
  - Close "back-of-bell option" (DPA) is mentioned in the audit only, with no numbers [2].
  - Reference point: the bell.
- **SAFETY:**
  - Weighted stands; no sharp edges at face or bell height.
  - Clear route for player, mute table and cables.
  - Never clamp a generic clip to the bell finish; keep valves, tuning slides and mute insertion unobstructed.
  - Mic electronics can overload before preamp meters show it [2].
  - Ribbon: follow that model's SPL, blast and phantom guidance.
  - NIOSH [7]; do not fire the bell at another person's ear.
- **MULTI-MIC:** For distant plus close, audition each and the mono sum (timing colours the tone). Check spot plus section in stereo and mono. No numbers.
- **APP PRESENTATION:** none. **AUDIO:** "No audio examples."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - No dedicated mic-selection section; no observation sheet.
  - DPA's distant view is quoted as "several-meter" here but "about 3 m" in the Trombone and Bass Trombone lessons (same source [2]).
  - The MDAT guide [Flugelhorn ref 4] names trumpet and flugelhorn together at 2–4 ft. The Trumpet lesson omits it, while the Flugelhorn lesson uses it.

- **MULTI-MIC:** Audition each mic and the mono sum. "Do not apply automatic delay or polarity inversion without hearing." Close unused channels during a trumpet/flugel switch.

### A02a Tenor trombone — Aerophones Lab, Tenor Trombone, October 4, 2026

- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Slide with "seven slide positions, with the seventh the farthest extension" [1]; slide described as "long, thin, precisely aligned" [4]; lower slide.
  - Bell, with a "stationary bell area" for a clip; mouthpiece.
  - Trigger (on a "trigger-equipped tenor"); plunger and other mutes.
  - Posture: standing/seated, instrument tilt, outward slide reach (the swept volume).
  - UNSPECIFIED: slide length, bell diameter, trigger tubing layout.
- **MICROPHONES:**
  - "Directional dynamic or suitable high-headroom condenser"; ribbon caveats.
  - Cardioid/supercardioid [5].
  - DPA 4099 bell-rim clip, "between the bell center and edge" [6]; mount "never on the slide".
- **POSITIONS:**
  - "30–50 cm (about 12–20 in) from the bell, slightly off axis" — DPA [2].
  - "1–2 ft (about 30–60 cm)" — Shure [3].
  - "about 3 m away" studio room view [2].
  - Practical stand location: "above or to the side of the slide's sweep, aimed across the bell" (no number).
- **SAFETY:**
  - Map the full slide sweep first; stand, boom, cable, wedge and people stay outside it.
  - "Never restrain, push or repair the player's slide."
  - Clip pressure and finish approved by player and manufacturer.
  - Overload [2]; NIOSH [7].
- **MULTI-MIC:** Each mic alone plus the mono sum; delay and polarity judged by ear. Section plus spot in mono.
- **APP / AUDIO:** none / "No audio examples."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - The doc explicitly labels slide-envelope mapping as "reasoned application" (inferred), not sourced; good practice.
  - No dedicated studio-vs-live comparison table; no observation sheet.
  - Dated Oct 4, after Bass trombone (Oct 3), although bass is "next".

- **APP / AUDIO:** none / "No audio examples."

### (b) Interaction patterns for one reusable engine

1. **Distance × axis on a radiator.** A bell (brass) or radiating zone moves a mic along distance and off-axis angle, with readouts "on-axis = brighter / off-axis = softer" and the sourced range bands drawn as zones:
   - DPA 30–50 cm
   - Shure 30–60 cm
   - MDAT 60–120 cm
   - DPA ~3 m
2. **Multi-zone radiator with register selector (woodwinds/saxes).** Bell plus tone-hole field; the active radiating hole moves with the note chosen. Positions to choose from:
   - bell-into
   - above-bell→holes
   - near-holes
   - 45° mid-body
   - one-third-from-bell at 15–20 cm
   - over-shoulder/room
3. **Clearance envelope.** Draw the player's swept volume and flag a collision if mic, stand or cable intrudes:
   - trombone slide sweep
   - horn turn / bells-up
   - accordion bellows arc
   - bass-clarinet peg
   - flute head turn
4. **Stand vs clip toggle.** Clip types: brass bell clip, sax clip, U-CLIP, A-CLIP, headset. Compare "consistency/isolation" against "acoustic perspective/feedback".
5. **Live-stage layer.** Wedge placement against the polar pattern (cardioid rear null vs supercardioid rear lobe), plus a spill source (drums).
6. **Signal-chain headroom check.** Capsule → pad → transmitter → preamp → converter; the meter "looks safe" while the capsule clips.
7. **HPF vs lowest required note** (Bass Tbn, Tuba, Euph, Bari, Bass Clar, Bassoon, Organ).
8. **Main + spot mono-sum check.** The same pattern appears in all 20.
9. **Source-path selector** (Harmonica: acoustic / cupped harp mic / amp speaker; Accordion: one mic / two sides / internal; Organ: stream vs PA).
10. **Mute/technique swap** (trumpet mutes, horn stopping, harmonica cupping).

Every lesson's "Musical aim → trial → listen for" table maps directly to a goal-picker preset list.

### (d) Disagreements between lessons

1. **DPA distant brass view:** "several-meter" (Trumpet) vs "about 3 m" (Trombone, Bass Tbn); same source.
2. **MDAT 2–4 ft trumpet/flugelhorn guide:** used for Flugelhorn, omitted for Trumpet although the guide names both.
3. **Tone-hole radiation wording:**
   - Flute: "first open fingerhole".
   - Sax lessons: "last open holes" / "lowest or last open holes" / "lower or last open holes … upper register".
   - Clarinet: bell "particularly for low fingerings".
   - The sax wording conflicts with the flute/clarinet physics as written.
4. **Supercardioid nulls:** "off-rear nulls" (Trumpet) vs "side nulls" (Piccolo, Sop Clar, Bass Clar, Bassoon). Woodwinds cite Shure's *Hypercardioid and Subcardioid* article for a supercardioid claim; the reference fit is doubtful.
5. **"One third from the bell":** "up from the bell" (Clarinet, Oboe) vs "down from the bell" (Bassoon). Consistent physically (the bassoon bell is on top) but needs per-instrument orientation in the engine.
6. **DPA 80 Hz transmitter low cut:** mentioned for Tenor and Bari only, not Alto or Soprano, though it is the same system.
7. **Shure "few inches" (sax) vs DPA metric (woodwinds):** unit systems differ (inches, feet, cm, m, "a few inches"). Display units need a decision.
8. **Header metadata:** brass and sax headers name the instrument; woodwinds, harmonica, accordion and organ say "Studio and Live Sound".
