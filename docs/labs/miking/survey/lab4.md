### C01 — Acoustic Guitar Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:** strings, bridge, soundboard/top, body, sound hole, 12th fret, neck-to-body joint (stated not to be the same point "on every guitar"), fretboard, upper bout, lower bout, upper soundboard. Variants: steel 6-string; 12-string with paired courses ("octave courses on the lower strings in common arrangements" [5]); nylon-string (Yamaha notes structural differences tied to string tension [6]). Onboard pickup / undersaddle system (UNSPECIFIED placement). Guitar clip mount. Player posture: sitting, standing, singing. Headstock, saddle and body depth are UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:** No model is recommended. Shure PGA27 appears only as the source guide [1]. Patterns discussed: cardioid/directional (proximity effect), omni, side-address condenser ("check the marked front"). DPA guitar clip [2, 8] (the 4099 mount guide, model implied). Stereo: coincident XY, near-coincident, spaced pair [7].
- **POSITIONS:**
  - "Near 12th fret or neck-to-body area, 15–30 cm away" [1, 2].
  - Shure: "6–12 inches (15–30 cm) near the 12th fret as a balanced starting position and closer to the sound hole for fullness" [1].
  - DPA: "between fretboard and sound hole for a body-mounted mic", angled toward the hole for more level [2]. No number given.
  - Capsule "aimed between upper soundboard and nearby strings" [1, 2].
  - Live (DPA): clip "aimed near the body/neck junction"; stage option "below the fretboard pointed away from a monitor" [2, 8]. No numbers.
- **SAFETY:** keep stand and capsule out of strumming, fretting and sight lines; never lean a boom into the player's face or instrument; a clip contacting the finish means stop and remove it with the player; route cable away from fingers, strap and feet; on feedback, lower level first. No OSHA or hearing note.
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:** XY gives a stable centre and good mono; spaced neck+body pair changes as the player moves; never aim both mics into the sound hole; solo each, compare at equal loudness, mono-check for comb filtering. "Move or rebalance rather than assuming a polarity switch or calculated spacing rule guarantees coherence." Pickup vs mic: compare each alone, then together in mono; DPA blend [8].
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** "No audio examples are included."
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part: 1✓ 2~ 3✓ 4✓ 5✓ 6✓ 7✓ 8✓ (exercise and pass criterion, no observation-sheet form) 9✓.
  - The 12th-fret vs body-joint ambiguity matters for the art. Steel-string guitars usually join the body at the 14th fret and classical guitars at the 12th. The lesson flags the difference but does not say which to draw.
  - No hearing-safety line, although live feedback is discussed.
  - No 12-string or nylon-specific sourced position; the lesson admits this.
  - Overlaps C07, which reuses the same DPA/Shure guidance, and E07 (singer with guitar).

### C02 — Electric Guitar Amplifier Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:** amp grille; loudspeaker with dust cap ("at the cone center"), cone, surround ("cone extending to its surround"); multi-speaker cabinet ("grille midpoint is not necessarily a speaker's center"); open-back vs closed-back cabinet; combo vs separate head/cabinet; hot vents/ventilation; tubes; speaker terminals; internal speaker jack (Fender example); chassis. Guitar, pedals and amp controls are named but not detailed (UNSPECIFIED). Speaker sizes, cabinet counts (2- or 4-speaker mentioned) and cone diameter are UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:**
  - Shure PGA27 guide [4] (no model specifics).
  - Sennheiser e 906 [2]: flat side-address, "designed to hang in front of an amp"; point the correct face at the speaker.
  - Types: dynamic ("common for close live work"), condenser, ribbon ("when specifications, mounting and power conditions suit").
- **POSITIONS:**
  - Start "near the border of dust cap and cone"; toward centre is brighter, toward edge is smoother [1]. e 906: between dome and cone edge [2].
  - "1–6 inches (2–15 cm) as a general amplifier range" [4]; stated as "not a minimum distance or a limit".
  - Rear of an open-back cabinet: Shure engineer example with polarity reversal [1]. No distance given.
  - Room mic: no distance given.
- **SAFETY:**
  - OSHA "85 dBA eight-hour time-weighted average" is an action level, not a safety guarantee [5].
  - Do not open the chassis, defeat the safety ground, alter speaker wiring or change the amp-to-cabinet connection.
  - Fender 65 Deluxe Reverb: speaker must be connected at the internal jack when the amp is on, "8-ohm load" [6]. Model-specific; do not generalise.
  - Stop on hum, damaged wiring, hot smell or smoke.
  - Verify phantom power and ribbon restrictions [7].
  - Never drape fabric over ventilation; keep cable away from hot vents; no stand contact with a moving cone or vibrating grille; keep stands clear of tubes and terminals; never reach inside the cabinet.
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:** second close mic on the same or another driver [1]; align capsules; solo each; mono-sum; "a switch cannot correct every frequency-dependent or room interaction". Front/rear polarity is "an example to test". Room mic: "no fixed 'three-to-one' rule or automatic time alignment guarantees a better musical result." DI / line / cab-sim output "is not equivalent to a microphone"; know speaker-level vs line-level first.
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part: 1✓ 2~ 3✓ 4✓ 5✓ 6✓ 7✓ 8✓ 9✓.
  - Content duplicates the Lab 1 "Amplified-source support module" (speaker-cabinet miking), which the master plan places in Lab 1. The overlap with C04, C08 and C12 is near-verbatim: same Shure [1]/PGA27 sources and the same centre-to-edge table.
  - No speaker or cone size is given for the art.

### C03 — Resonator Guitar and Dobro Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Strings, bridge, one or more metal resonator cones, coverplate, openings / sound ports, body.
  - Three distinct designs: spider-bridge single-cone, biscuit-bridge single-cone, and tricone [1, 2] ("do not present identical sound fields").
  - Square-neck lap-style (strings and coverplate face upward) vs round-neck upright.
  - Steel bar / slide, picking hand, spider screw, neck/body side.
  - Fishman cone-mounted pickup [5].
  - Coverplate hole patterns, port locations and cone diameter are UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:**
  - Shure KSM32 and KSM44A: used by Jerry Douglas when a mic is used [3]. Patterns not stated in the lesson.
  - "A cardioid condenser can reveal detail; a dynamic can provide a robust close option."
  - DPA 4099G, explicitly listed for guitar and Dobro; clip "body-depth fit of 35–122 mm" [6].
  - Electrical paths (not mics): Fishman Aura Jerry Douglas imaging pedal [3, 7]; Fishman Classic Series Active Resophonic pickup (spider/biscuit variants; professional installation) [5].
  - Supercardioid rear-lobe caution.
- **POSITIONS:**
  - "Roughly 20–45 cm (8–18 in) from the instrument, facing the broad coverplate/upper-body area from slightly off the player's picking path." Labelled trial: "not a manufacturer-specified optimum".
  - Regions: off-centre coverplate/cone; neck/body side and strings; sound ports or a broader frontal view; very close localised spot. No numbers for any of these.
  - Lap style: boom from the side.
- **SAFETY:**
  - Do not open the coverplate or adjust the cone, bridge, spider screw or pickup. The cone is delicate [2].
  - Keep booms and clamps off resonator hardware; never attach to the cone or obstruct the coverplate or ports; get owner consent before any clip touches the finish.
  - Bar, arm or instrument movement must not be able to strike the mic.
  - OSHA 85 dBA [8].
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:** second mic for a distinct purpose; mono check; "reposition and rebalance before assuming a polarity switch". Pickup vs mic vs both in mono: "no fixed delay or polarity setting suits every instrument". An imaging-pedal output is electrical and must be labelled that way.
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part complete except 2~.
  - The only number in the lesson is a trial.
  - Three resonator designs plus two playing postures make this costly to illustrate.
  - Shure's flat-top sound-hole chart is explicitly not transferable.

### C04 — Pedal Steel and Lap Steel Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Signal chain: strings → magnetic pickup → volume pedal → effects → amplifier → loudspeaker [1].
  - Pedal-steel mechanics: foot pedals, knee levers (mechanical pitch change), bar, fingerpicks, neck(s) ("which pickup/neck").
  - Lap steel: no pedals or knee levers.
  - Amp: grille, dust cap, cone, speaker. Tilt foot (Peavey caution) [4]. Amp XLR, effects-loop send, preamp out [4].
  - Acoustic hollow-neck lap steel is mentioned as an exception (no geometry).
  - Pedal and lever counts, neck count and frame/legs are UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:** no model. "Suitable directional mic"; dynamic for stage; condenser if its SPL rating and pad suit. Peavey Nashville-Session 112/115 XLR is a "*microphone-simulated direct interface*" [4]; the lesson says this is electrical, not a mic.
- **POSITIONS:**
  - Dust-cap/cone boundary [2].
  - "General amplifier trial range of 2–15 cm (1–6 in)" [3]. Explicitly "not published steel-specific coordinates".
- **SAFETY:**
  - "Never connect a speaker output to a microphone preamp or ordinary DI input."
  - Use speaker cable, not shielded instrument cable [4].
  - Do not disconnect or substitute speakers, open the chassis, defeat the protective earth, change mains wiring or block ventilation.
  - Speaker-free operation is model-specific [4]. Peavey warns about an overlong tilt foot that lets the cabinet fall when bumped [4].
  - Stop for smoke, abnormal heat or shock symptoms.
  - OSHA 85 dBA [5].
  - Keep the stand and cable out of the volume-pedal and knee-lever zone.
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:**
  - Two-mic and front/rear examples [2]; rear only on an open back; polarity is "a test, not a universal fix".
  - Pre-amp DI preserves a dry path but omits reverb, EQ, power stage and speaker. Preserve pickup loading; no casual parallel splits.
  - Mic arrives later than an electrical tap: move and rebalance, then polarity/timing.
  - "Neither a fixed spacing rule nor automatic alignment is guaranteed."
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part complete (2✓ via "Tonal concepts and microphone choices").
  - Duplicates the C02 amp content.
  - The pedal steel is one of the hardest instruments in the lab to draw accurately. It may not need full detail, because the miked source is the amp, but the lesson's pedal/knee-lever clearance zone requires drawing it.

### C05a — Banjo Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Strings, bridge, tensioned head (membrane), rim/pot, neck/pot junction ("neck joint varies by banjo"), tailpiece.
  - Resonator back vs open back [1].
  - Five-string (primary), tenor and others to be tested. Fingerpicks, clawhammer, mute.
  - Fifth-string peg, flange, tension hooks and head diameter are UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:**
  - DPA 4099 miniature and compatible banjo clip (VC4099 page lists banjo compatibility) [2, 5].
  - DPA: two omnis; cardioid XY; wider A/B [2].
  - Shure engineer: "figure-eight ribbon on one banjo to smooth its attack", ruled out for wedges [4]. Model not named.
- **POSITIONS:**
  - DPA neck/body junction [2]. One-mic trial "roughly 30–40 cm (12–16 in)", borrowed from DPA's two-omni stereo distance; "a teaching trial, not a published requirement".
  - Shure live: "about 1 ft (30 cm) from the bridge" [3].
  - Shure miniature "attached near the strings between bridge and tailpiece" [3]. The lesson cautions against clamping without an approved mount.
- **SAFETY:**
  - Never rest a mic or clip on the head, change head tension, disturb the bridge or tighten resonator hardware.
  - "Shure's engineer reports measuring 105 dB near a particular banjo [4]". The lesson calls it anecdotal.
  - OSHA 85 dBA [6].
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:** XY limits arrival-time differences; spaced pair gives different width and phase; mono check; move or rebalance before polarity. Pickup vs mic in mono: timing, polarity, feedback. Shared bluegrass mic is cross-linked to Lab 5.
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part 2~.
  - The 105 dB figure has no weighting, distance or time base.
  - The 30–40 cm one-mic trial is a re-use of a stereo-pair distance.
  - The banjo is a membrane radiator but is classified as a chordophone. The lesson cross-links Lab 1 and says it is not a guitar.

### C06a — Plucked Upright Bass Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Strings, bridge, carved body/top, f-holes, fingerboard, endpin, soundpost, tailpiece.
  - Outer strings below the bridge (BC4099 mount point).
  - "Open E1 is about 41 Hz"; low extensions and extra strings go lower [1].
  - Bassist position. Scroll, ribs and instrument height are UNSPECIFIED.
  - Fishman Full Circle pickup [4]: installed, location UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:**
  - DPA BC4099 clip, attached "to the two outer strings below the bridge … carries a gooseneck" [1, 5].
  - Patterns: directional (proximity effect), omni (DPA recommends for full-instrument capture), supercardioid rear lobe.
- **POSITIONS:**
  - Shure: "stand mic 15–30 cm (6–12 in) in front of the instrument above the bridge"; "another option a few inches from an f-hole for fullness" [2]. "A few inches" has no number.
  - DPA: "area under the bridge, between strings and top" for a mounted mic; aiming closer to the f-hole gives more output [1]. No number.
  - Higher toward the fingerboard; farther overall view. No numbers.
- **SAFETY:**
  - Shure warns against clipping directly on the bridge [3].
  - No improvised foam wedge or cable tie.
  - Keep stands and cables away from the endpin and the bassist's feet; never let a stand tip onto the instrument.
  - Do not remove or reposition the bridge, soundpost, tailpiece or pickup.
  - OSHA 85 dBA [6].
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:**
  - "Avoid an arbitrary three-to-one spacing rule."
  - Mic receives delayed airborne sound vs the pickup: placement and balance first, then polarity/timing; "a polarity button cannot align all frequencies".
  - Keep channels separate.
  - Fishman Full Circle needs an impedance-matching preamp and professional installation [4].
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part 2~.
  - "In front of the instrument above the bridge" is ambiguous for art: it could mean higher than the bridge on the instrument face, or a height. It must be resolved before drawing.
  - Overlaps heavily with C06b; same refs [1], [2] and the same 15–30 cm.

### C06b — Bowed Upright Bass Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:** as C06a, plus the bow (bow hair, frog, tip), the bowing arm and "complete trajectory" / "full bow arc", bridge wheels, varnish, neighbouring basses (section). Orchestral section seating is UNSPECIFIED.

- **MULTI-MIC / DI:** spot vs main pair in mono; "polarity button cannot correct all phase differences"; the spot need not carry the low foundation; Fishman Full Circle [8].
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**

### C08 — Electric Bass Amplifier Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)
- **ANATOMY TO DRAW:**
  - Bass: fretted vs fretless fingerboard, pickup, active/passive electronics.
  - Chain: pedals → preamp → power amp → cabinet.
  - Cabinet: woofer(s), dust cap, cone, grille, horn/tweeter (optional), multiple driver sizes (15-inch vs 10-inch are mentioned as a non-rule), rear ports, open backs.
  - Amp head: speaker jack, line/DI XLR, Pre/Post switch [1].
  - DI box with THRU, XLR, pad, low-cut (Radial J48) [5].
  - Lowest E "about 41 Hz".
  - Cabinet layouts (for example 4x10, 1x15) are UNSPECIFIED.
- **MICROPHONES:** no mic model. "Directional mic"; "close dynamic and farther condenser possibilities" [2]. Check the mic's LF response and SPL handling [2]. Radial J48 DI "needs 48 V phantom power" [5].
- **POSITIONS:**
  - Dust-cap/cone boundary; centre gives more bite/brightness and edge is warmer/darker [2].
  - Shure bass article: "10–45 cm (4–18 in) … for more breathing room" [2].
  - General amp guide: "2–15 cm (1–6 in)" [3].
- **SAFETY:**
  - Never patch a speaker output to a mic input, line input or ordinary DI.
  - Speaker cable for head-to-cab; never defeat a protective earth.
  - Ampeg tube-output models generally require a load (model-specific) [6].
  - Do not open the chassis or bypass interlocks.
  - Radial: power off or levels at zero when making or breaking DI connections [5].
  - Keep away from hot vents. OSHA 85 dBA [7].
- **MULTI-MIC / DI:**
  - Mic alone, DI alone, then mono blend; acoustic path arrives after the DI; "time adjustment may help in a fixed studio arrangement"; "no universal delay or polarity setting".
  - Shure recommends checking phase between DI and mics [2].
  - J48 low-cut "approximately 6 dB down at 80 Hz", "not an automatic switch for a standard bass whose lowest E is about 41 Hz".
  - "Do not expect a polarity button or fixed three-to-one spacing to solve all interactions."
- **PROPOSED APP PRESENTATION:** none.
- **AUDIO:** none.
- **QUALITY FLAGS:**
  - 9-part 2~.
  - 5-string low B (about 31 Hz) is not mentioned, even though the J48 80 Hz filter discussion would apply even more strongly.
  - The phrase "post-amp-preamp DI" is awkward.
  - Overlaps C02/C04/C12 and the Lab 1 speaker module.

### C09a — Violin and Fiddle Miking Technique (Oct 3, 2026)

### (b) Interaction patterns one reusable lab engine could serve
1. **Instrument + draggable capsule over named regions** → readout "Likely result / Check before choosing". Every lesson ships this 3-column position-map table, so it can be one data-driven component. **18 of 20 lessons** use acoustic regions; C02, C04, C08 and C12 also use cone regions.
2. **Speaker-cone lateral slider** (centre ↔ dust-cap/cone boundary ↔ edge) + off-axis rotate + distance; multi-driver choice; optional horn and open-back rear. Used by **C02, C04, C08, C12**. One "cab" engine; it could equally serve the Lab 1 speaker module.
3. **Distance band with provenance badge.** Sourced bands (for example Shure 15–30 cm) are shown differently from "classroom trial" bands. Every lesson separates the two. This is the clearest way to meet the calculator-accuracy rule.
4. **Clearance envelope overlay** (bow arc, picking/strumming arc, risha arc, pedal/knee-lever zone, lid travel, endpin, player's head). A capsule inside the envelope is an unsafe fail state. Used by **all 20**.
5. **Polar pattern picker + wedge placement** (cardioid rear null vs super/hyper rear lobe). Every live section uses it.
6. **Two-path blend / mono check** (mic+mic, mic+pickup, mic+DI, spot+main): delay/polarity toggle with comb-filter visual. The order "move/rebalance → then polarity/timing" is identical in **all 20**.
7. **Signal-path labeller** (strings → pickup → pedal → DI tap → amp → speaker → mic). The student marks electrical vs acoustic paths and gets an unsafe-patch stop (speaker output into mic pre). Used by **C02, C03, C04, C07, C08, C12**, plus the pickup lessons.
8. **Mount fit checker** (body depth 35–55 mm / 35–122 mm; string diameter 0.6–2.3 mm; owner consent). Used by C03, C05a–c, C06a–b, C07, C09a–c, C10.
9. **Stereo pair builder** (XY, ORTF, spaced omni, near-coincident) with mono fold-down. Used by C01, C05a–c, C10, C11, C13, C14, C15.
10. **Main array + spot fader** with an "image pull" warning. Used by C06b, C09a–c, C10, C11.
11. **Symptom → first check → response cards.** All 20 have a troubleshooting table.
12. **Observation sheet / change-one-variable log.** Only C13–C15 list the fields explicitly; all 20 imply it.

Engine is constrained by house rules: no stylized audio; no audio examples, per the plan. Any sound or comb-filter display must therefore be a real computed response, or the lab must have no sound at all (owner decision).

### (d) Disagreements between lessons
1. **Clavinet "aim the rejection null away from the loudest monitor"** contradicts every other lesson (C03, C04, C05c, C06a, C13, C14, C15), which say to point the monitor into the rejection region. Clavinet is wrong.
2. **Amp distance:** 2–15 cm (PGA27, used in C02, C04, C08) vs 10–45 cm (Shure bass article, C08). C08 explains the difference; C02 and C04 never mention the wider band.
3. **Small plucked "trial" ranges are inconsistent**, with no stated rationale:
   - ukulele 20–40 cm;
   - resonator and acoustic bass guitar 20–45 cm;
   - banjo and mandolin 30–40 cm;
   - sitar A 25–45 cm;
   - oud and veena A 35–60 cm.
4. **Bowed strings:** violin and viola use an unsourced trial of 0.5–1.2 m; cello uses Shure's sourced 30 cm; bowed bass uses Shure's 15–30 cm. Without labels, the app would present a 4× distance jump within one family.
5. **Hearing safety parity:**
   - OSHA 85 dBA is cited in C02, C03, C04, C05a–c, C06a–b, C07, C08 and C09a.
   - It is absent from C09b, C09c, C10, C11, C12, C13, C14 and C15.
   - Clavinet is the only amp lesson without it.
6. **Unit order varies.** Most give cm first; C02 and C10 give inches/feet first; C07 gives "about 12 inches" with no metric conversion.
7. **Two templates and date styles** (see §1). C13–C15 use lettered methods and observation fields; C01–C12 do not.
