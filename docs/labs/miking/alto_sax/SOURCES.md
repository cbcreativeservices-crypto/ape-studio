## 1. Radiation physics — resolves the survey's "last open holes" flag (A05a–d)

| Fact | Source words | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Where the lesson wording comes from | Dean Giavaras: "For any notes where all the keys aren't closed (high notes especially), much of the sound comes from lowest or last open sound holes on the body of the horn." (S-SAX) | High (it is a quote) |
| Physics | "If you open the tone holes, starting from the far end, you make the pressure node move up the pipe, closer to the mouthpiece… the first open tone hole acts approximately as though the saxophone were 'sawn off' near the location of the tone hole… in practice the wave extends somewhat beyond the first open tone hole: an end effect." (UNSW-SAX) | High |
| Same physics, measured | oboe: "frequencies below the cutoff are radiated from the first open holes. Above the cutoff frequency the sound is mostly radiated from the further open tone holes and the open end." (PL-2010 §4.2) | High |
| Bell as filter | "The bell cuts in at 1.8 kHz for the tenor and 2.6 kHz for the soprano. Above these frequencies the instruments have hardly any resonances – but they do behave as good megaphones!" "As far as fundamental frequencies go, the bell is really only important to the lowest notes. However, it is important to the high harmonics of most notes." (UNSW-SAX) | High |
| Spread | "The sound of a saxophone is fairly well distributed between the finger holes and the bell… Any open hole on the instrument also produces sound… the saxophone radiates sound in a much wider pattern than brass" (S-SAX) | High |

**Verdict / proposed lesson wording:** keep Giavaras's words only as an attributed quote. The lesson's own
sentences (Sop L6 "lowest or last open holes", Alto L6 "lower or last open holes, especially in the upper
register", Tenor L6 "last open holes", Bari L6 "last open holes") → **DIFFERENT**; replace with: "For any
note, most of the sound below the tone-hole cutoff leaves through the **first open tone hole** (the open hole
nearest the mouthpiece) and the open holes just past it; with every key closed (the lowest notes) it leaves
through the bell. As the pitch rises within a register, that first open hole moves up the body toward the
mouthpiece. High harmonics also come out of the bell." Consistent with Flute ("first open fingerhole") and
Bassoon (Yamaha) — survey 3d-3 resolved.
