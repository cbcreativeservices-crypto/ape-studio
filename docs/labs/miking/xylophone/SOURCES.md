## b. Mallet guidance (lesson L7, L46, L79)

| Source | Exact words | Verdict for the lesson |
|---|---|---|
| YMH-CARE ([6]) | "Never use metallic mallets on marimbas, xylophones or vibraphones because they can damage or break the bars." "Never use rubbing alcohol (isopropyl alcohol) on marimba or xylophone wood bars"; "moisture is the archenemy of bars" | CONFIRMED |
| YMH-DECON ([3]) | "Always remember that the mallet should never be harder than the bar of the instrument. For example, on rosewood xylophones, use plastic, not metal." | CONFIRMED (hardness/click/wear) |
| YMH-MAL ([4]) | "Hard rubber is suited to instruments such as the glockenspiel and the xylophone"; Lexan/ABS/Hytrel "suited to the glockenspiel and the xylophone" | CONFIRMED |
| YMH-JENKS-ARC ([5], archived) | "The characteristic "crisp" xylophone sonority is enhanced with medium hard rubber or polyball mallets. Plastic mallets damage bars and must be avoided!" | **CONFIRMED, and STRONGER than the lesson says**: Jenks bans plastic outright; YMH-DECON recommends plastic on rosewood xylophones. XYL-D2 = a direct contradiction between two Yamaha documents (the lesson's "differ in specificity" is generous). |
