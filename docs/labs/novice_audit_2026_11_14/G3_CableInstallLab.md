## CableInstallLab: overall verdict
This lab looks and feels the most professional of the three cable labs: nearly every stage is a drawn scene with a decision, and the feedback is excellent (scored route verdicts with + / − reasons, the twist meter on the coil, the "honest result" style ✕ lines with a FIX). A beginner who starts gets hooked by Stage 1's "which install would you approve?" and Stage 3's "shortest route finished last". But the lab is written for people who will work in buildings: from Stage 6 on, the vocabulary (reverse-racked, plenum, AHJ, firestop, riser, T-bar grid) and the long colour-key paragraphs pile up, and some drawings are too small on a phone for their many rings and markers. A beginner would likely enjoy stages 1-4 and 9-10, slow down hard at 6, 8 and 11, and come back for the coil and the labelling story.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Long legend / colour-key paragraphs placed ABOVE the drawings (stages 6, 8, 9, 11), and a colour key that changes on stage 8. The beginner reads engineering notes before seeing the task.
2. Feedback that lands out of sight (stage 6's find explanations below the rack) or answers given away (stage 1's card titles "THE PROFESSIONAL", stage 2's lock-on-first-pick reveal).
3. Building-trade vocabulary with no one-line glossing at first use (raceway, plenum, rated wall, riser, OD, pull, fill), and the "Cable dressing" glossary card that says the opposite of the lab's first sentence.

**3 best things**
1. Scoring every option, not just the pick (stage 3's six bars per route and "the shortest pull finished LAST").
2. The over-under coil with a live TWIST meter (stage 9).
3. Problem-before-rule design (stage 1's four racks, stage 12's four identical unlabelled cables).

**First 30 seconds, rewritten**
Open on Stage 1's four close-ups, untitled A-D, with one line: "A contractor says all four are finished. Only one would pass a professional inspection — and it is not the prettiest. Which one?" After the pick, reveal the nicknames and the reason drawn on the picture, then: "This lab teaches you to see what the inspector sees: route, support, protection, separation and labels." The INTRO paragraph and regulatory note move behind SOURCES / STANDARDS.

Source check (after the walk): `registry.ts` confirms 13 stages with the titles seen. The rack find counter (`RackScene.tsx`, `FoundCounter`) counts up with an animation, which explains why it stayed at "0 found" in a background preview tab; I did not count that as a finding.
