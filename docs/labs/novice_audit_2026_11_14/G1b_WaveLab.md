# G1b · Wave Physics Laboratory (WaveLab) Modules 7-16 — on-screen, hands-on beginner audit

Auditor G1b, 2026-11-14. Follow-up to G1_WaveLab.md, whose Modules 7-16 were judged from text only. G1's word findings are NOT repeated here; this file adds what the screen shows: layout, crowding, what the eye meets first, what the controls do, and what the CHECK YOURSELF feedback looks like (each answered wrong first, then right). Sound OFF throughout (no module here needs sound).

**How I looked (decisions, made without asking):**
- The Browser pane was at its tab cap (9 tabs). G1 had finished all its labs (G1_PROGRESS.md), so I reused G1's old tab `tab-4` (it showed RoomDesignLab) rather than touch another auditor's tab. Deep link: `http://localhost:8142/?g1b=N#labpreview/WaveModule/<id>`.
- The pane is short: at 390 x 844 the screenshot cuts off the bottom ~75 px of the phone (the dock). So I looked at the screen at **390 x 770** (whole phone visible, dock included) and took every height measurement at **390 x 844** with the page's own layout numbers (getBoundingClientRect). The display and dock are fixed-height, so the only difference between the two sizes is the height of the reading window, which I give for 844.
- Taps by accessibility ref; sliders by drag. Layers I turned on, I turned back off.
- Dragging the listener / sources on the room plan: the browser tool's drag did not move them in the scaled phone emulation (the canvas uses a touch responder). I moved them with a scripted slow mouse drag (mousedown, 12 small moves, mouseup) at the icon's real position, which is what a finger does. Not a finding about the app.

**Cross-module screen findings (apply to every module below; numbered S1-S10 so entries can refer to them):**
- **S1. The reading window is a letterbox.** On a 390 x 844 phone the fixed top (header + display + readout strip + HIDE/FULL SCREEN row) ends at y = 491, and the dock + open fader tray start at y = 719. All the module's teaching text, tables, second graph, COMMON MISTAKES and CHECK YOURSELF scroll through a **228 px window (27 % of the screen)**. Module 7's well is 1,658 px tall: about 7 window-fuls. At a 770-tall phone the window is 154 px (about 6 lines of body text). A beginner reads through a slot. (SHARED: rack layout.)
- **S2. A fader tray is open by default and cannot be closed by tapping its key.** The first dock key's fader (e.g. WALL) sits open above the dock from the first frame, costing ~50 px of the reading window. Tapping the key again leaves it open. (Module 14 has no fader key, so no tray; its window is ~283 px at 844, the roomiest in 7–16.)
- **S3. The room plan is small; the things you are told to move are dots.** The plan occupies roughly the top third of the display (~130 x 100 px on the phone). Speaker and mic/listener are ~6 px marks with no labels (G1 noted the missing labels; on screen the real problem is also SIZE: a 0.15 m nudge moves the mic ~5 px). The eye goes to the big yellow graph instead, which in most modules is the right place, but the "cause" (geometry) and the "effect" (graph) are not visibly linked. (The touch radius is 26 px in code, so dragging works; the issue is seeing.)
- **S4. The screen guide (ⓘ / EXPLAIN THIS SCREEN) is the best beginner text in the lab, and it is hidden behind an icon.** Five short pages (WHAT YOU'RE SEEING · WHAT YOU CONTROL · WHAT CHANGES AND WHY · WHAT THIS TEACHES · TRY THIS), each highlighting the part it talks about. Its card jumps between the top and the bottom of the screen from page to page, so the eye has to chase it. (SHARED: screen guide.)
- **S5. No way back to the starting point.** The intro card and the guide's TRY THIS quote start values ("the mic is 0.50 m from the wall: Δt ≈ 2.91 ms"). After a few presses those numbers are gone, and there is no RESET key; leaving and re-entering the module is the only way back. (SHARED: dock.)
- **S6. CHECK YOURSELF feedback is good.** A wrong pick turns red and shows a one-line HINT (not the answer); the right pick turns green, adds ✓ to the heading and shows a full explanation that points back to the control to try. On a 770-tall phone the hint lands below the reading window (you see the red outline but must scroll to find why). (SHARED.)
- **S7. A chooser that "stays open — compare while you watch" sits on top of the reading window.** LAYERS / MODE / ROOM choosers open as a band over the bottom of the well (~80 px), leaving ~70 px of readable text at 770 tall and ~150 px at 844. Multi-fader panels (ARRAY in 10, ROOM in 16) cover all of it. A tap meant for a CHECK YOURSELF answer under it lands on the chooser instead (it happened to me in Module 8: the answer did not register until I closed the chooser). (SHARED: DockTray.)
- **S8. Readouts change at once; the map redraws 1–2 s later.** In Modules 8 (MODE), 10 (FREQ) and others, the numbers update instantly while the heat map keeps the old picture for 1–2 s (web preview; may be faster on a phone). For a moment picture and numbers disagree. Module 8 also shows an empty box with only wall labels for ~1–2 s on arrival. (SHARED: wave display.)
- **S9. The reading window often opens on a grid of number tiles, not on words.** Modules 11 (8 tiles), 12 (7), 13 (8), 14 (11), 15 (5) put a dashboard of numbers first, mostly repeating the strip; the one plain paragraph comes after. Modules 10 and 12 open on an uncaptioned REAL HARDWARE photo (208 px, nearly the whole window). The first thing read under the picture should be one plain sentence.
- **S10. The best orientation devices are inconsistent across modules.** On-picture labels exist only in Module 12 (REAR MIC, FRONT SUB, AUDIENCE ↓) and partly 14/16 (wall materials); a named-place chooser with a plain description exists only in Module 14 (CANYON / GYM / CHURCH / WAREHOUSE); a permanent "before" reference exists only in Module 15 (the dim all-concrete decay line); a named "wrong" button exists in 11 (THE WRONG TOOL) and 12 (WRONG WAY). Each is excellent; each should be in every module.

## Verdict — WaveLab Modules 7–16, seen on screen (adds to G1's lab verdict)

**Beginner's journey.** On screen these ten modules are much friendlier than their text: each opens on ONE striking picture (a breathing comb, a rainbow pressure map, a torch-like cone, a tilting beam, an echo timeline, a decay slope), and one or two taps make it change in a way a newcomer can see (the comb spreads, the quiet stripe appears under your chair, the cone narrows, the beam leans, a stem turns red past 50 ms, the slope shortens). The trouble starts below the picture: everything a beginner should read scrolls through a 228 px letterbox (27 % of a 390 x 844 screen), often opening on a grid of number tiles or an uncaptioned photo, and the open fader tray and "stays open" choosers eat into it further. The things you are asked to move are tiny unlabelled dots, the colour keys live in captions far from the maps, and the excellent screen guide is behind an ⓘ. Room Builder then asks for all of it at once on a crowded five-key dock.

**3 biggest on-screen orientation problems**
1. The letterbox reading window (S1, S2, S7, S9): 228 px for 900–2,600 px of content, starting with numbers, not a sentence.
2. Pictures without on-picture words (S3, S10): unlabelled source/mic/listener/sub/main dots, heat maps with no colour key (and "bright = loud" when yellow-green is brightest, Module 8), graphs with no axis words (7, 15), side view not marked as side view (10).
3. Cause too small, effect too big: the cause (mic move, delay staircase, room size) is drawn at a few pixels while the effect fills the display; the link between them is left to the beginner (7, 13, 16), and in some cases the picture barely changes while a readout flips (10 BOXES, 12 WRONG WAY).

**3 best things on screen**
1. One big, changing picture per module with an instant reaction to one tap (comb, pressure map, coverage cone, beam steering, echo timeline, decay slope).
2. CHECK YOURSELF feedback: hints that send the eye to the exact place ("Check the 50 ms marker on the timeline", "Compare the array length readout to the wavelength readout") and capstone hints that name the module that taught the idea.
3. The orientation devices already invented in single modules (S10): on-picture labels (12), named real places with a plain card (14), a permanent "before" line (15), named wrong buttons (11, 12), plain fader values ("1/4 walls fiberglass", 15).

**First 30 seconds rewrite (for any module 7–16).** The display shows the picture with every object named on it (SPEAKER, MIC, YOU, SUB, MAIN) and a three-word colour/axis key. Under it, in place of the tile grid, one sentence and one button: e.g. Module 7: "A wall bounces a copy of the voice back into the mic. Press MIC → and watch the dips move." → [MIC →]. After the first press, a faint ghost of the old picture stays for a second, and one line appears: "Every dip moved: you moved the problem, not the tone." Numbers, tables, model notes and the second graph sit below, behind "Go deeper".

**Pages audited here:** 10 (Modules 7–16), all on screen at phone width, every dock key and chooser tried, 13 CHECK YOURSELF questions answered wrong then right (one each in 7–15, four in 16). Not re-audited: the WaveLab home, Modules 1–6 and What's left (G1 saw them on screen).
