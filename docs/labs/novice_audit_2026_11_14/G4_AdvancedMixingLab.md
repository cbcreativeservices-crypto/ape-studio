## Lab verdict · Advanced Mixing (20 pages + end screen walked)

**The beginner's journey.** For someone who has just done Beginning Mixing, this lab feels like a real step up with a familiar rhythm (bold idea, cards, picture, ▶ comparisons, check, goal chips). Several pages are excellent for a learner even without sound: Phase (live comb graph), Stems (timeline above cards), Stem Reconstruction (one-button null test with ✓/✗), Mix Diagnosis (everyday complaints), Auxiliary Routing (paths stay lit). The burden is vocabulary and density: almost every page opens with a lede that names three to five advanced concepts, and five pages add a "toolkit" of four or five more terms at the bottom. For a total beginner who opens this lab first, it is a wall (overwhelm 4-5 on most pages). With sound off, about a third of goal chips are unreachable and the lab cannot be finished.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Each page carries two lessons: the main experiment and a 4-5 item "toolkit" (pages 7, 8, 10, 11, 13) — too many new ideas per page for a learner.
2. Dense ledes and paragraphs with nested parentheses and standards jargon (page 4 WHEN EACH TOOL WINS, page 6 setup, page 15 BS.1770 disclaimer, page 14 MASTERING BORDER).
3. Drawings that start in an empty / BYPASSED / "—" state (pages 8, 14) or measurements that only appear after audio (page 6 PEAK/RMS/CREST), so a silent learner sees an apparently broken instrument.

**3 best things**
1. Predict-then-reveal structure: cases and checks that explain wrong answers and fill the picture only after the learner commits (pages 9, 17, 19, 20).
2. Continuity: the page-2 priorities, the same 8-track session and routing map from page 3 through the page-20 repair; callbacks to Beginning (double route, naming, mono).
3. Real-world stories in plain words: "the handoff from hell", the club mono system, the client asking for −8 LUFS, the recall a year later.

**First 30 seconds (rewrite suggestion).** Page 1 top line, large: "Beginning Mixing taught you to make a clear mix. Here you'll manage, fix and deliver a big one — 20 short pages, ending with repairing someone else's broken mix." Then the three-check gate. Then, on page 2, the priorities contract with "Page 20 will show these back to you."
