### Detour before module 1 · UNDERSTANDING LEVEL & AMPLITUDE (color-language primer)

The three-question CHECK YOURSELF is excellent (tap the warmer colour; it ends "You just read three displays — one you had never seen."). Suggestions: (1) **Tell the learner it is a one-time 1-minute detour and they will return to Seeing Frequency** — the header line replaces the module title, so a beginner thinks they opened the wrong lesson. **S, SHARED.** (2) Drop the six tool names to captions like "picture of sound over time" for this first meeting. **S, SHARED.** (3) "That is the skill" claims a skill. **S, copy.**

## Lab verdict — EQ Lab (EqLabHome)

**Beginner's journey:** The menu is the calmest entry in my set — a numbered 15-lesson path with a reassuring "each one introduces only what the next needs". The first click takes the learner on an unannounced detour (the colour-language primer) and then into the most technical page (live spectrum with HPF / RES / FFT readouts), so the first two minutes are harder than lessons 2–6, which are genuinely beginner-friendly (Why We Use EQ, the Camera Analogy, Q & Bandwidth, Filter Shapes). The middle (Slopes, Graphic EQ phase view, Live Spectrum) spikes in jargon; the TRAIN pages (Find, Match, Fix) restore confidence with tasks and feedback. The end screen shows progress well but does not sum up what was learned.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Order: module 1 (live spectrum + mic, jargon readouts) is harder than module 2; the primer detour is unannounced.
2. Jargon spikes in 7, 10, 11 (Butterworth, poles, phase, dBFS, FFT, COMB) with no plain-word line.
3. CHECK questions give the answer away by length (the correct option is the long one with a built-in reason) — same five questions as EqLab.

**3 best things**
1. One-control pages (Why We Use EQ, Q & Bandwidth) — a model of "one idea at a time".
2. The Camera Analogy: picture and curve move together.
3. TRAIN pages with CHECK / SCORE feedback and real complaints ("BOOMY KICK").

**First 30 seconds rewrite**
Menu top line: "15 short lessons. Start at 1 — about 2 minutes each. If you have never used an EQ, do the one-page *Equalizer* lab first." Then lesson 1 = today's "Why We Use EQ" ("An equalizer turns parts of a sound up or down. Tap LOW and drag GAIN down: you just cut the bass."), and the live-room spectrum becomes lesson 2 with "Tap MIC to look at your room's sound".
