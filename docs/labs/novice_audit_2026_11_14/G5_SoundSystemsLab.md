## LEARN pages (the pager reads MODULE n / 23; chapters 1-14)

Frame for every LEARN page: header, module bar, a display (system map / coverage plan / console model), a readout row, HIDE DISPLAY / FULL SCREEN, one instruction line, then a folding LAB NOTES block (CHAPTER n · title, a paragraph, ▸ a prompt, ○ goals, a "THE RULE" box), then NEXT: … and the dock. The dock has no SOUND or STOP key on these pages (none needed). The display is labelled honestly ("SYSTEM MAP — ILLUSTRATIVE", "CONCEPTUAL COVERAGE — ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL").

Shared problem found on page 2: the dock key on "Trace the signal" is called **NEXT ▸** (a chooser), while the page button at the top is also **NEXT ›**, and the instruction says "Open NEXT and tap the stations…". A beginner will press the top NEXT and leave the page. Also, on arrival the intro card sits over the page; a tap on the top NEXT while it is open only closes the card (it took me two taps to move on each page): a beginner will think NEXT is broken (SHARED, intro card behaviour).

### Pages 8-23 (read in full, main control tried on a sample; condensed to the fields that differ)

Format per page: **First look · Overwhelm (1-5) · Knows what / what to do · Words · What works · Suggestions (size)**. Hobbyist line at the end of the block.

**Page 8 · Subwoofer feeds (ch 5).** First look: a "SUB FEED ROUTER" diagram (six channels → buses → processor/XOVER 80–120 Hz → LOW/HIGH), blue = what reaches the sub. Overwhelm 4. **Page 9 · Sub placement and arrays (ch 5).** First look: venue plan with a blue low-frequency glow. Overwhelm 4. Words: power alley, cardioid, end-fire, modes. Works: "Low frequencies are long: a 60 Hz wave is nearly six meters" is a memorable physical fact; honest "measure before you trust a layout". Suggestions: 1. **Page 10 · Stage monitors (ch 6).** First look: stage plan with wedge, side fill, drum fill, in-ears; "people enlarged for clarity". Overwhelm 3. **Page 11 · Monitor console, splits and talkback (ch 6).** First look: MONITOR SPLIT diagram (isolated splitter → FOH console / monitor console). **Page 12 · Wiring and system connections (ch 7).** First look: six proposed connections with CONNECT / REFUSE, then a LEVELS table. Overwhelm 3; game-like and clear. **Page 13 · Amplifier loads (ch 8).** First look: one amp channel with cabinets in parallel; the wire turns gold, then red. Overwhelm 4: the formula "Ztot = 1 ÷ Σ(1/Zi)" and a bridging paragraph. **Page 15 · System setup and deployment (ch 9).** First look: 16 numbered steps. Overwhelm 2. **Page 18 · Delay time, alignment and polarity (ch 11).** First look: mains plus delay towers 30 m back; a ring shows the wavefront. **Page 19 · Processing and system tuning (ch 12).** First look: nine processing blocks with where each lives, then an 8-step tuning session. Overwhelm 4 (a list page, no display interaction). **Page 20 · Feedback control (ch 13).** First look: performer, mic pattern, wedge, a dashed loop "microphone → console → amplifier → wedge → air → microphone". Overwhelm 3. **Page 21 · Testing and troubleshooting: the method (ch 14).** First look: a symptom quote ("The PA is dead — but every channel meter is dancing.") and a probe-state legend. Overwhelm 3. ## SOUND SYSTEMS LAB · overall verdict

**The beginner's journey.** A newcomer is greeted by a plain definition of "sound system" and a hub that says "LEARN teaches, the other four make you do it". LEARN's first chapters (the system map, trace the signal, kinds of system, match the venue) are inviting and well paced, with quizzes built from real venues. From chapter 5 (subwoofers) onward the pages assume vocabulary (crossover, aux, comb filtering, impedance, dBu) and show formulas in the lab notes; a total beginner would stall somewhere between pages 8 and 13. The other four modes are excellent once LEARN is done: BUILD's live/silent scoring, ROUTE's four-word rule, OPERATE's "who is listening" question and TROUBLESHOOT's customer-voiced symptoms all teach method, not just facts. The lab ends LEARN with a true recap ("What you can now do") and a no-score check.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. **Jargon and formulas arrive before the plain idea** (chapters 5-12): crossover, comb filter, impedance, dBu, SPL and delay formulas in the notes, often with no one-sentence plain version first.
