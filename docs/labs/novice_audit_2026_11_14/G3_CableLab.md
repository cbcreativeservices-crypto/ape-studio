# G3 · CableLab (Cable & Connector Fundamentals): beginner orientation audit

Walked 2026-11-14 at 390 x 844, sound off, tab-7, `http://localhost:8142/#labpreview/CableLab`. The lab has no home screen: it opens straight on Lesson 1, and a strip under the title moves between 11 module positions (12 lessons; the readout "1 / 11 ▾" opens CONTENTS).

Shared things met on the very first open (they apply to every page below, so they are said once here):
- Open order: (1) a large glossary card "Cables (audio)" (term intro) covering the page, (2) after CONTINUE, a second card "WHAT YOU'RE LEARNING / WHERE TO START" (screen guide intro) with GOT IT and EXPLAIN THIS SCREEN, (3) after GOT IT, a coach tip "New: tap ⓘ to have this screen explained" next to the ⓘ.
- **The coach tip "New: tap ⓘ…" sits right on top of the NEXT › button of the module strip.** My first tap on NEXT › was swallowed by the tip (module stayed 1 / 11); the second tap worked. A beginner thinks NEXT is broken. (SHARED)
- Two different "next" controls on every lesson: NEXT › in the top strip (module) and a green NEXT: <LESSON NAME> › at the bottom. Plus "LESSON 1 · 1 OF 12" against "MODULE 1 / 11": two numbering systems that do not agree (12 lessons, 11 positions). A beginner asks "am I on 1 of 11 or 1 of 12?"
- "0 UNITS CLEARED" in the top right never says what a unit is or how one is cleared.

## CableLab: overall verdict
A curious beginner gets a strong start (the "what travels?" quiz) and, in lessons 4, 8 and 10, some of the best-designed learning in the app: one question, a picture drawn to scale, a fair hint, an honest consequence. Between those, lessons 2, 3, 5 and 6 read as reference manuals: connector records longer than the phone screen with pin tables, ⚠ notes and fold-outs, dozens of standards names, and the action (the knowledge check) at the very bottom. The lab's big idea ("the plug does not prove the connection") is excellent and repeated on every page, but the beginner drowns in vocabulary before it lands. Most would finish lessons 1 and 4, stall in lesson 3's XLR record, and might never reach the tester or the challenge, which they would enjoy most.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Reference-card density on lessons 3, 5, 6 (and 7): each connector record is longer than the phone screen, and the beginner cannot tell the must-know line from specialist detail.
2. Core words left undefined at first use: "level" (mic / line / instrument / loudspeaker), "balanced", "powered vs passive", "protocol", "OPEN / CLOSED". Each is the hinge of its lesson.
3. Navigation and progress cues that disagree: two "next" buttons, "MODULE 1 / 11" vs "LESSON 1 · 1 OF 12", "0 UNITS CLEARED" never explained, quiz counters that sometimes show a total ("ROUTING PICKS · 1 OF 5", "SIGNAL PATH · 1 OF 9") and sometimes not ("PAIR 2", "LOOK-ALIKE 1"), check headings in three styles ("0 SOLVED", "SOLVE ALL 4", "4 QUESTIONS"), and the "New: tap ⓘ" tip covering NEXT ›.

**3 best things**
1. Lesson 4's look-alike pairs with "THE HONEST RESULT OF CHOOSING WRONG".
2. Drawn to scale everywhere (plug line-up, cut ends, peel), so the pictures can be trusted.
3. Lesson 8's decision chain and Lesson 12's WHAT'S LEFT with links back.
