# G8 · Microphone Selection Lab (`#labpreview/MicSelect`)

Walked 2026-11-14 at 390 x 844, member, sound off (this lab has no audio at all, so nothing was lost by sound off). 12 screens: 9 lessons, the challenge, the mic locker, the what's-left end screen. Every lesson's intro card ("WHAT YOU'RE LEARNING / WHERE TO START") was met fresh; the full screen guide (4 steps) was read on Lesson 1 and the rest checked in `micSelectScreenGuides.ts` after the walk.

## Shared frame of this lab (judged once)
- Header: "MICROPHONE SELECTION LAB / Types, Characteristics & Applications", ⓘ guide key. Strip: ⏮ · ‹ PREV · MODULE n / 11 ▾ · NEXT ›. Then an amber italic line on every page: *"What microphone should I choose for this job — and why?"* — the best orientation sentence in the lab: one question the whole lab answers.
- Each page: "LESSON n · n OF 11" over a title, a one-line question, then content, then a big NEXT: <next lesson title> › button at the bottom (a good "what's next" cue).
- Naming wobble: the strip says MODULE, the page says LESSON, page 10 says CHALLENGE, page 11 OPTIONAL. A beginner wonders if "module" and "lesson" are different things. (Owner flag, not a suggestion: the strip "1 / 11" and "1 OF 11" are position-in-sequence readouts; check them against the "no N of total" rule if that rule is meant to cover position as well as progress.)
- Intro card sits mid-screen over the content each time a lesson opens. It is clear and short. Tapping outside it closes it but the tap is swallowed, so a beginner who taps NEXT while the card shows sees nothing happen and has to tap again (seen on Lesson 6).
- No sound, no rack, no dock: the calmest lab frame in the app for a beginner.

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | The intro card. Behind it: a big amber-bordered warning paragraph, then a 4 x 3 grid of mic photos. The eye reads the page as "a catalogue of microphones". |
| Overwhelm | 3. The first block of real content is the hardest paragraph in the lab: "These are NOT equivalent categories. 'Condenser' names the transducer principle inside; 'shotgun' names an acoustic directivity design; 'lavalier' names a physical form factor…" — before the beginner has seen a single mic card. 12 photos with 9 px names follow. |

| Knows what to do first? | Yes, thanks to WHERE TO START ("Read the Moving-Coil Dynamic card…"). Without the card: they would tap a photo, which is also right. But the card appears BELOW the 12-photo grid, out of view at 390 x 844; tapping a photo changes something off screen with no visible feedback. |
| Words they would not know | transducer, transducer principle, acoustic directivity, form factor, electret, capacitor capsule, polarizing voltage, diaphragm, side-address, shock-mounted, interference tube, off-axis, comb filtering, boundary, SPL, sensitivity, passive, transient. |
| Follows / continues? | Mostly. COMPARE trap: tapping COMPARE empties the card and says "Pick two microphones to place them side by side"; the Moving-Coil Dynamic that was showing is not kept as pick one, so the WHERE TO START instruction takes three taps and the result appears off screen. Two near-identical photos (Condenser and Large-Diaphragm Condenser) confuse a beginner who is told they are different categories. |
| What works | Real photos; every card ends on an IMPORTANT LIMITATION ("no type is best at everything"); TYPICAL APPLICATIONS in plain jobs (live vocals, drums, guitar amplifiers). The ⤢ photo zoom. |
| Suggestions | 1. Move the "NOT equivalent categories" paragraph BELOW the photo grid, or shorten it to one plain line: "Some names say what's inside the mic, some say its shape. Group the grid in two labeled rows: "HOW IT WORKS INSIDE: Dynamic · Condenser · Electret · Ribbon" and "SHAPE OR JOB: Large-diaphragm · … · Contact" — the picture then teaches the very point the warning paragraph makes in words (M). 5. Use a visibly different photo for "Condenser" (e.g. a generic condenser symbol or a small-body mic) so it is not a twin of Large-Diaphragm Condenser (S). |
| Hobbyist | Knows dynamic vs condenser; will enjoy Boundary/Contact/Measurement. May find the category warning pedantic but useful. |

## Page 2 · Lesson 2 · CHARACTERISTICS THAT MATTER

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | Title, then a condenser photo with 16 chips beside/below it. Reads as "a list of spec words". |
| Overwhelm | 4. Sixteen terms at once, most never met before; each card is two lines of dense text. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Partly: "the characteristics that actually drive a selection". They think they must learn 16 definitions. |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes: Polar pattern is already open; the card says tap each chip. |
| Words they would not know | Polar pattern, frequency response, sensitivity (mV/Pa), Maximum SPL ("acoustic level", "specified distortion limit", "dB SPL" — SPL is never spelled out as sound pressure level), self-noise ("dB(A)"), signal-to-noise ratio ("94 dB SPL reference"), equivalent noise level, output impedance, preamp, phantom / bias, transient, off-axis, bleed, proximity effect, feedback. |
| Follows / continues? | They can tap through, but retention will be low; nothing asks them to use any term here. |
| What works | "'Dynamic vs. condenser' is nowhere near enough information" is a strong hook; the amber myth-busters ("Flat is not synonymous with better", "Higher sensitivity does not automatically mean a better microphone"); Weight and Ruggedness cards are human and memorable ("A boom operator holds this mic overhead for hours"). |
| Suggestions | 1. Group the 16 chips into four labeled groups so the page has a shape: WHERE IT LISTENS (pattern, off-axis, proximity) · HOW IT SOUNDS (response, transient) · QUIET AND LOUD (sensitivity, max SPL, self-noise, SNR, ENL) · BODY AND RIG (impedance, power, size, weight, ruggedness, environment) (M). 2. Tell the beginner which ones the next lessons cover ("Lessons 3–6 take the first four in depth; just skim the rest") so they do not try to memorise 16 (S). 3. Spell out SPL at first use: "Maximum SPL (sound pressure level: how loud)" (S). 4. Put the self-noise trio together in one card: "three ways to state the same hiss" (the guide says it; the screen does not) (S). |
| Hobbyist | Useful checklist. Will stop at mV/Pa and dB(A) and wonder what number is "good"; one typical range per spec would help them. |

## LAB VERDICT · Microphone Selection Lab

**Beginner's journey:** The single framing question ("What microphone should I choose for this job — and why?") and the per-lesson intro cards keep a beginner oriented, and the lab gets better as it goes: the polar-pattern stage, the source scale, the challenge and the locker are clear, hands-on and give real feedback. The first two lessons are the hardest part: Lesson 1 opens with a taxonomy warning in jargon and Lesson 2 drops sixteen spec terms at once, so a newcomer may feel lost exactly where they decide whether to continue. Without sound it loses nothing; there is none.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. The hardest words come first: the "NOT equivalent categories" paragraph (Lesson 1) and 16 ungrouped spec chips (Lesson 2).
2. Things change off screen: tapping a mic photo or CHECK MY REASONING updates a card below the fold with no scroll; COMPARE drops the current mic.
3. Small labeling gaps that stop a beginner: A/B/C chips with no names, a graph with only Hz numbers, an unanswered capitalised question in Lesson 9, "phantom" never defined on screen.

**3 best things**
1. One question for the whole lab, repeated on every page, and a NEXT: <title> button at the end of each.
2. Lesson 3's stage drawing with live ✓/⚠/△ tags and reason feedback.
3. The challenge's partial-credit verdicts and "change one variable", and the no-penalty end check.

**First 30 seconds (rewrite suggestion)**
Lesson 1 opens on the photo grid, split into two labeled rows ("HOW IT WORKS INSIDE" / "SHAPE OR JOB"), with the line: "Tap a mic to see what it's good at — and what it's not. Names describe different things: a 'shotgun' is usually also a 'condenser'." The first card (Moving-Coil Dynamic) shows directly under the row the beginner tapped.
