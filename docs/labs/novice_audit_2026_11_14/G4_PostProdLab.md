| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | Purpose ("Copy the material in, prove the copy is good, and leave the originals exactly as you found them. This is the least interesting stage in the lab and the one that most often decides whether the project survives."), WHY THIS MATTERS, a SAFETY box ("Never reformat, delete or overwrite an original card … This is the one irreversible action in post-production."), then Protect the Originals and verification questions. About 1,090 words, 10 required — the shortest stage. |
| Overwhelm | 3. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Yes, and it is told why it matters in honest words ("the least interesting stage … decides whether the project survives"). |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes (intro: set sources to cleared). |
| Words they would not know | ingest, "read-only", "Locked", checksum, "truncated", "reformatted", "sample rate conversion". |
| Follows / continues? | Yes. "No, the sources have been cleared" → BLOCKED · "Sources were cleared before the copy was proven … If anything did not come across, there is nothing left to go back to." | What works | The SAFETY line as the single irreversible action; blocker logic that teaches order (verify, then clear). |
| Suggestions | 1. (S) Gloss checksum in place ("a fingerprint of the file's content; if the fingerprints match, the copy is identical"). 2. (S) After a related action fails to clear a blocker, say why in the blocker text. |
| Hobbyist | Very valuable; card-wiping before backup is a classic home-studio loss. |

## Stage 3 · Build and Synchronize the Session (fresh project)

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | Purpose ("get every recording lined up against the same clock"), WHY THIS MATTERS ("the person most inconvenienced by a session nobody can read is usually you, three weeks from now"), LEGAL, then Session Configuration and sync questions. About 1,270 words, 9 required. |
| Overwhelm | 4. Session numbers (sample rate, bit depth, frame rate, start time) are abstract for a beginner. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Partly: "set the session up right and keep things in sync". "Sync" and "drift" are explained only through the findings. |
| Knows what to do first? | Partly. The intro card's first step happens in ANOTHER stage ("Set Stage 1's 'Delivery sample rate' to 48 kHz. Back here, set…"). On a new project, choosing 44.1 kHz here produced no finding (because Stage 1 was empty), so the step taught nothing until the learner leaves and comes back. |
| Words they would not know | sample rate, bit depth, frame rate, "start time / timecode", "independent recorders", "clock", drift, "flam" (glossed), "reconforming". |
| Follows / continues? | Yes. "How many independent recorders: Several" → "Several recorders on their own clocks … over an hour it is a flam — a doubled, smeared hit — … Check sync at the END of the longest recording". Clear and memorable. |
| What works | The drift explanation (short take inaudible, an hour = a flam); glossing "flam" in place. |
| Suggestions | 1. (S) When a check depends on another stage, show the dependency here ("Delivery sample rate (from Stage 1): not set ›"), so the comparison happens on one screen. 2. (S) A tiny two-waveform picture for "drift" (aligned at the start, apart at the end) would make sync concrete. |
| Hobbyist | Drift between a phone recorder and a camera is a real hobbyist pain; well taught. |

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | Purpose ("make the joins invisible. This is where a pile of material becomes a thing with a shape."), WHY THIS MATTERS ("a flawless recording of nothing in particular"; "the repair becomes the new damage"), LEGAL (editing speech can change meaning), then source review, edit and repair questions. About 1,000 words, 6 required — compact. |
| Overwhelm | 2-3. Fewer questions; language is musical and human. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Yes: "choose takes by performance; edits should be invisible; don't over-repair". |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes (intro: alternates → Removed). Note: the same word "Removed" is an option in two questions (alternates and breaths), so a learner following "set … to Removed" can easily tap the wrong one (I did, and got the breath finding instead). |
| Words they would not know | takes, alternates, "room tone", "pickup", "repair", "level-matched". |
| Follows / continues? | Yes. Breaths → Removed: "Speech without breaths does not read as polished; it reads as machine-made…". | What works | The LEGAL note on editing speech (ethics of editing, rarely taught); findings that explain how listeners perceive edits. |
| Suggestions | 1. (S) Quote the full question in the intro card ("set 'Are the alternates still available' to Removed") — it already does, but the option label alone is ambiguous on screen; consider "Removed (alternates deleted)" vs "Removed (every breath)". 2. (S) Gloss room tone at first use ("the quiet sound of the room with nobody talking"). |
| Hobbyist | Podcast hobbyists will learn the breath and room-tone lessons immediately. |

## Exercises (eight; a podcast project shows 1-5 and 7; a live project shows all eight) — all opened on screen

Shared layout identical to Pre-Production exercises (scenario, project type, OPEN THE PROJECT AND FIX IT, "N STILL TO DO" checklist, READ THE REASONING, START AGAIN); the shared findings and suggestions there apply (no goal reminder inside the stage; reasoning revealed on demand). Post-production differences:
- The button reads "OPEN THE PROJECT AND FIX IT" here and "OPEN THE PLAN AND FIX IT" in Pre-Production — fine, but say "project" or "plan" consistently across the two labs.
- Scenario vs project-type mismatch: on a "Live performance or event" project, exercises 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7 all describe "an episode" or "an interview" (podcast scenarios) under a chip that says "Live performance or event". A beginner will wonder which world they are in.
- Checklists are longer (5-8 items) and more technical than Pre-Production's; exercises 7 and 8 have 8 items each.
- Hands-on: in exercise 1 (Clarify the handoff) I typed an approver in "Who approves the final" and came back; the "One named person is recorded as the approval authority" line did NOT tick. I cannot tell whether that is the shared-browser storage wipe (the exercise screen re-reads the saved project) or a mismatch between that field and the check; worth a clean check on a phone. (Pre-Production exercise 1 did tick in the same test.)

Per exercise:
- 1 · Clarify the handoff — "a cheerful note saying 'the usual'" is a perfect beginner hook; 6 checks.
- 2 · Safe ingest — duplicate and truncated files, mixed sample rates; 5 checks; "verified by more than a file count" is the key idea.
- 3 · Restore synchronization — three recorders, one a frame or two late by the end; 5 checks; "frame" is unexplained for an audio beginner.
- 4 · Repair without overprocessing — "Two of those decisions will make the program worse than leaving the problem alone"; 7 checks; ADR unexplained.
- 5 · Add without erasing — four additions that each sound "slightly wrong"; 7 checks covering rights, matching, beds, perspective, ducking music under speech, tuning consent. Overwhelm 4.
- 6 · Route the mix (live only) — find three structural faults; 6 checks; ties to Stage 6 and Advanced Mixing.
- 7 · Meet the specification — the spec is stated in the prompt (−16 LUFS ±1 LU, −1 dBTP; measured −13.2 LUFS, +0.4 dBTP, LRA 2 LU), which solves Stage 7's cross-stage problem; 8 checks including captions and accessibility. Overwhelm 4.
- 8 · The upload succeeded, but delivery failed (live only) — "the job was marked done eleven days ago"; 8 checks; the strongest real-world story in the two production labs.

## Lab verdict · Audio Post-Production (start + home + 8 stages + 8 exercises + packet walked; stages on fresh projects because of the shared-browser storage wipe)

**The beginner's journey.** Post-production feels better organized than pre-production from the first screen: the eight stages sit in three named chapters (Prepare and edit · Build and mix · Finish and deliver), which tells the story of the job. Each stage opens with a vivid WHY THIS MATTERS paragraph ("Almost every post-production disaster is a question that was answerable in week one and got asked in week six") and then a long form whose findings teach real craft in plain consequences (a wiped card before verification, no room tone, tuning without consent, upload is not delivery). The burdens are the same as Pre-Production — long single-scroll forms, stacked legal/safety boxes, a gap list at the bottom — plus two specific to this lab: intro instructions that start in another stage (Stages 3 and 7), and percentages that can drop after answering.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Cross-stage dependencies (sample rate, loudness spec) are set in Stage 1 but judged in Stages 3 and 7, with no read-only view of the Stage 1 values where they are needed; a new project's checks therefore stay silent (e.g. a measured +0.4 dBTP raises nothing).
2. Same long-form load as Pre-Production: 1,000-1,500 words and up to 30 "Not applicable" buttons per stage; LEGAL/SAFETY boxes stacked before the first question.
3. Exercise scenarios (episode, interview) shown under a "Live performance or event" chip; progress percentage that falls after an answer; exercise goals not visible inside the stage.

**3 best things**
1. Chapters on the home screen and a WHY THIS MATTERS paragraph per stage that a beginner will remember.
2. Findings that teach perception and ethics, not only rules (breaths, room tone, editing speech can change meaning, tuning consent).
3. Honest options ("I am assuming the usual", "Nothing has been approved yet") and sanity checks (a positive true-peak ceiling is "usually a typed sign or the wrong unit").

**First 30 seconds (rewrite suggestion).** "You've been handed a folder of recordings. This lab walks you from that folder to a delivery the client has accepted, in three chapters: prepare and edit, build and mix, finish and deliver. Pick what you're working on, then follow NEXT OUTSTANDING — it always points to the most useful next question." Show the home in neutral colours until the learner has answered something.
