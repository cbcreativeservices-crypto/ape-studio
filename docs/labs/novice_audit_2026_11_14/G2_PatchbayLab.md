| Field | Finding |
|---|---|

| Overwhelm | 2. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Yes. Card: "A half-normal pair starts out exactly like full-normal. Here you predict, then test, what a plug in each jack does to its normal." |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes: "Answer PREDICT BEFORE PATCHING first; the jacks stay locked until you do." BUT: I answered WRONG ("It goes silent, exactly like full-normal") — feedback: "Reasonable guess — that is exactly what full-normal would do. Try the top jack below and watch the normal." There is NO top jack: the dock stays hidden, the status still says "Commit to your prediction above first — then the jacks unlock", and the PREDICT IT chip stays unticked. Only after I picked the RIGHT answer did the dock (TOP JACK / BOTTOM JACK) appear. So the page says "predict, then test", but in practice it is "guess until right, then test" — and the wrong-answer text sends the student to a control that is not there. | Words they would not know | half-normal, tap ("The top jack becomes a tap of the source"), split. |
| Follows / continues? | After the right answer, yes: TOP JACK showed the normal staying green AND the cord getting a copy; status "SPLIT — CONSOLE OUT 1 feeds INTERFACE IN 1 AND the patch cord", both chips ticked. A wrong guesser may quit here. |

## PatchbayLab — overall verdict

The beginner's journey: this is the most carefully taught lab of my six. One rule (top = source, bottom = destination; a normal is what happens when you do nothing) is stated on page 1, built up one idea per page, recapped at page 13, scaled to a whole bay, applied to real problems and ended with the same mantra. The best devices in all my labs are here: predict-before-patching, task chips that tick, wrong answers that name the misconception, and pictures that appear as a reward. The weak spots: page 1 has no picture of a patchbay at all; 23 pages with no stopping point is long on a phone; several pages are text-only quizzes in a row (8, 10, 11, 12, 18, 21, 22, 23); and the page-7 prediction lock leaves a wrong guesser with an instruction to tap a control that is not there. Device names and abbreviations multiply in part two (TAPE RTN, MONITOR CTRL, COMP IN, ALT SOURCE, outboard, send/return, bantam).

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. No patchbay picture on page 1; the beginner learns "top row / bottom row" with nothing to look at.
2. Page 7: a wrong prediction keeps the jacks locked while the feedback says "Try the top jack below"; only the right answer unlocks them.
3. Length and pacing: 23 pages, no resting point, and the end screen reports "15 pages still to go" without saying what is missing on each.

**3 best things**
1. Predict-before-patching (pages 7, 11) and "Rule out one wiring per test" (page 12): real reasoning, not recall.
2. Self-ticking task chips and the "WORDS YOU WILL HEAR" vocabulary box.
3. The page-13 recap and the closing mantra: the lab tells you where you are in it.

**"First 30 seconds" rewrite (page 1)**
Show the 8-pair bay picture first with one pair outlined and an arrow down: "This is a patchbay: every cable in a studio ends here. Top row: sound comes OUT of gear. Bottom row: sound goes IN. Say it: top is out, bottom is in." Then one tap: "Tap the top jack" (as on page 2), so the first page already has a hands-on moment. Add "This lab has two parts: one pair (12 pages), the whole studio (10 pages). You can stop after part one."
