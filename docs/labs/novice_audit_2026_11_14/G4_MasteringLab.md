### M2 · Step 3 · Which role? (PRACTICE) — shared practice layout

Same card quiz layout as M1 step 4 (judged there). Difference: answers are longer sentences ("Add a transient shaper on the whole master and ride it per section") with one jargon term each, so the learner may pick by elimination of unknown words rather than by reasoning. The right answer's explanation is strong ("Any program-wide move at the snare's frequencies drags the vocal and guitars with it. The element lives in the mix."); the wrong answer gets the same generic "not the best fit here" line. Overwhelm 2. Suggestion: (S, SHARED) specific wrong-answer reasons; (S) keep answer options free of new jargon (or gloss "transient shaper" inline). Hobbyist: quick.

### M2 · Step 4 · Review — shared review layout

Same as M1 step 5. KEY IDEAS (3), KEY TERMS (Translation, Stems), TAKEAWAY ("Same ears, different perspective, different control." — a memorable line). Credit line: "answer its 4 decisions on the PRACTICE step (1 so far)". Overwhelm 1. Same suggestions (tap-to-reveal terms; show the decision count on the practice step). Note: Module 2 has 4 steps (no LISTEN step), Module 1 had 5; the "STEP n / N" readout changes per module, which is fine.

**Module 2 summary.** Short, clear, mostly reading; the EXPLORE step (request picker that lights the flow) is the highlight. Risk of feeling repetitive after Module 1 steps 2 and 4.

| Overwhelm | 3. The drawing has six small labels at phone size; the eight cards are long ("Room modes and boundary effects set the bass…", "about 38% of the way from the front wall to the rear wall… a 5.0 × 4.3 m room with 330 × 400 mm monitors"). |

| Words they would not know | HVAC, "noise floor", "tails", "Early reflections", "first-reflection points", "stereo image", "Room modes", "boundary effects", "bass trap", "diffusion", "equilateral triangle", "full-range", "subwoofer". |

### M3 · Step 2 · Monitoring level and listening habits

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|

| Overwhelm | 4. The graph is an equal-loudness model ("ISO 226-style model, simplified · vs 83 dB SPL (C)"); the notes quote NIOSH and OSHA rules, A- vs C-weighting, pink noise at −20 dBFS RMS. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Partly → yes after the fader: "quiet listening sounds thin, loud sounds full". |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes: ride MONITOR. Dragging to 55 dB gives BASS −6.4, TREBLE −1.4, READING QUIET and a plain sentence ("a quiet listen reads thin"). Strong cause → effect, fully silent. |
| Words they would not know | dB SPL, (C), "slow", "equal-loudness", ISO 226, NIOSH, OSHA, dBA, "C-weighted", "pink noise", "−20 dBFS RMS", "calibrated SPL meter", "fatigue". |
| Follows / continues? | Yes. |

| Hobbyist | The calibration recipe is directly useful to them. |

### M3 · Step 3 · Build a monitoring path (EXPLORE)

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | A chain heading "PLAYBACK → CONVERSION → LEVEL → AMPLIFICATION → AIR", an empty glass ("EMPTY CHAIN · Nothing connected yet."), readouts STAGES 0/5, TO ADD DAW, PATH EMPTY; dock: DEVICE selector, + CONNECT, ‹ UNDO, CLEAR. Thinks: "a building game". Good. |
| Overwhelm | 3. The instruction paragraph is long and technical ("USB from the computer only into the converter … balanced line after conversion, the red-and-black speaker pair from a power amplifier"). |
| Knows what it is learning? | Partly: "the order of boxes from computer to speakers". |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes: + CONNECT. But pressing + CONNECT six times in a row auto-builds the CORRECT chain (DAW → DAC → MON CTL → POWER AMP → speakers, "PATH COMPLETE") because the selection "moves on to the next unplaced device by itself". The learner can complete the step without making a single decision. The useful feedback (e.g. after DAC: "No loudspeakers yet: nothing turns the signal back into sound") appears, but no choice was tested. |
| Words they would not know | DAW, DAC / conversion, MON CTL / monitor controller, POWER AMP, PASSIVE / ACTIVE loudspeakers, USB, "balanced line", "upstream", "bass management", "subwoofer". |
| Follows / continues? | Yes. The second task (CLEAR, build one ending in ACTIVE) is where a real choice happens (an active pair makes the amplifier "a mistake"), and is the better exercise. |
| What works | Live grading of the chain (CHECK THE CHAIN / WORKS · MINIMAL / COMPLETE) with reasons; the power-order safety tip ("Amplifiers … on LAST and off FIRST"). |
| Suggestions | 1. (M) Do not auto-advance the DEVICE selection on the first build; make the learner pick the next box (or offer two candidates) so order is actually decided. 2. (S) Add a one-word job under each device icon (DAC = "turns numbers into sound waves", MON CTL = "volume knob") on the glass. 3. (S) Shorten the instruction paragraph; move cable types to ⓘ. |
| Hobbyist | Active vs passive is a real confusion; the second build teaches it. |

### M3 · Step 4 · Room and monitoring decisions (PRACTICE)

Shared card layout (see M1 step 4). Card 1 is a model question ("you listened to the second one 6 dB louder. What do you know?" → "Nothing yet — match the levels and listen again"; explanation: "A comparison at different levels tells you about the level, not the decision."). A beginner reasons this out from step 2: good spacing of learning and practice. Overwhelm 1. Same shared suggestions.

### M3 · Step 5 · Review

Shared review layout. KEY IDEAS (4), KEY TERMS (Room modes, Monitor controller, Listening fatigue), TAKEAWAY. Credit line says 5 decisions. Overwhelm 1. "Amps on last, off first" is a practical safety line worth keeping in the review.

**Module 3 summary.** The most practical module for a home learner. Two strong interactive displays (level fader → thin/full; chain builder), but each sits on top of very long, standards-heavy notes (NIOSH/OSHA, ISO 226, calibration with pink noise) that a beginner cannot use yet. The chain builder auto-completes itself on repeated + CONNECT, so the first build tests nothing.

### M4 · Step 3 · Analog and digital (LEARN)

Three short cards (What it is / What it is not / How to compare honestly). Overwhelm 1. Clear, reassuring for a beginner who cannot afford gear ("Excellent masters are made entirely in the box and entirely out of it"). Words: "analog domain", "in the box", "recall". Intro asks to "List the four things you would match" — nothing on screen collects that list (no feedback). Suggestion (S): turn the four comparison conditions into four tick boxes or a one-line answer reveal. Hobbyist: welcome myth-busting.

### M4 · Step 4 · Choose a tool for the job (PRACTICE)

Shared card layout, but here each card offers EIGHT options (Broad EQ, Narrow corrective EQ, Gentle compression, Peak limiter, Stereo width / M-S tool, Level trim + loudness meter, Phase correlation / mono check, Listen on another system first) — far more than the three in other modules, and most are jargon to a beginner. The inclusion of "Listen on another system first" (a check, not a tool) is a lovely teaching move. Overwhelm 3. Suggestion (M): show 3-4 options per card (the right one, one plausible, one check, one wrong) instead of all eight. Not answered here (moved on); the review then read "7 decisions … (0 so far)".

### M4 · Step 5 · Review

Shared review layout; KEY TERMS Tilt EQ, Limiter, Mono compatibility. Overwhelm 1. The intro card asks "name the seven tool groups from memory" — a heavy recall load for a beginner after a 25-name catalogue. Suggestion (S): ask for three ("Name the tool for tone, for loudness and for checking").

**Module 4 summary.** The organizing idea (tools grouped by the decision they serve) is the right one for beginners; the delivery (25 tool names, five expert displays, eight-option quiz) is calibrated for hobbyists.

### M5 · Step 2 · Inspect the delivery (labelled PRACTICE in the NEXT button)

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | A realistic inspection sheet for "NightSignal_Mix_v7_FINAL.wav" with ⚑ flags (version mismatch v7 vs v8, −0.1 dBFS "Almost no headroom", DC offset, a bus limiter note, "CD-R for the launch party"). Thinks: "a detective file". Engaging. |
| Overwhelm | 3. Every row has a technical value; the flags are what carry meaning. |

## Lab verdict · Mastering Lab (8 modules, 39 steps + end screen walked)

**The beginner's journey.** The lab opens with the best beginner picture I met in this run (many mix lines merging into one stereo master) and a calm, repeating five-beat rhythm per module: SEE/LEARN → EXPLORE (one dock control that changes a picture) → LISTEN → PRACTICE cards → REVIEW. Concepts are introduced in a sensible order (what mastering is → roles → room → tools → workflow → loudness → release → a full fictional EP). The EXPLORE steps are excellent and fully silent-friendly (request picker, monitor-level fader, chain builder, signal presets, playback systems, running order). The weak points are the LISTEN cockpits (a full limiter/EQ faceplate, gain-reduction graph and six meter cells with standards notes, reached as early as Module 1 step 3), notes written standards-first (BS.1770, ITU-R, NIOSH/OSHA, ISO 226) rather than plain-first, and many new acronyms shown in drawings without glosses. Without sound the three LISTEN steps teach little; everything else works.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. LISTEN steps (M1 S3, M5 S3, M6 S2) present the full professional cockpit and 150-word mechanics paragraphs to a beginner; the plain "habit" line is at the bottom.
2. Names drift between intro card, dock list and readout for the same option (STEREO / "Stereo / spatial"; QUIET MID / "Quiet song in the middle"; the intro asks for options the step does not open on).
3. PRACTICE feedback for wrong answers is generic ("not the best fit here … does not address what the situation actually asks for") — the mixing labs' specific wrong-answer reasons are much better teaching; plus the practice length (8 cards) is only revealed on the review step.

**3 best things**
1. The opening "many tracks → one stereo file" picture and the CAN / CANNOT framing, reused by every practice card and the final EP.
2. EXPLORE steps with one control, an immediate visual change and a plain one-line reading (e.g. Kick drum "Huge peak, tiny average"; Phone "Mono and almost no low end").
3. Real-job framing: the inspection sheet with a v7/v8 mismatch, client quotes per destination, FACT vs EXAMPLE labels, and the fictional EP with a "keep the quiet song quiet" trap.

**First 30 seconds (rewrite suggestion).** Skip the glossary popup or merge it into the intro card. Show the merge picture with one sentence: "Mastering is the last step before release: one finished song file in, one release-ready file out. You'll learn what it can and can't fix — in eight short modules, each ending with a few practice cards." Then tell them the one control: "Drag the gold STAGE handle at the bottom from MIXING to MASTERING."

Technical item noticed (not orientation): M6 step 1 Kick drum and Organ presets show TRUE PK below sample PEAK (Kick: PEAK 1.6, TRUE PK −5.0), contradicting the on-screen definition and M6 step 5's card. Worth a check.
