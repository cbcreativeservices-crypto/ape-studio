## Lab verdict — Room Design & Monitoring

**Beginner's journey.** The lab opens with a page of text about kinds of numbers and hearing safety, which delays the fun. From Module 2 it becomes the most practical lab in my set: a recognisable floor plan of "my room" with a desk, two speakers and a person, a green L−R check, a coloured bass map that shows the chair sitting in a dead spot, and a treatment step whose ABSORB NEXT button picks the next panel for you. The language is honest and careful (every result tagged CALCULATED / ESTIMATED / MEASURED), but readouts and captions use acoustician shorthand (S:M:L, (1,0,0) axial, EARLY <15ms, RT60 never defined), and the Review page buries its excellent personal advice under tables.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Module 1 is all text with no picture and no action; the "three kinds of number" lesson comes before the learner has any numbers.
2. Shorthand readouts and labels (RATIO S:M:L, (1,0,0) AXIAL, EARLY <15ms, W2/W3, "wall 4"), and RT60 used throughout without a definition.
3. The Review page order (tables first, TRY FIRST far below) and a contradictory count (6 vs 2 early reflections); plus the double meaning of FINISH.

**3 best things**
1. A realistic floor plan of the learner's own room that they build and drag.
2. SUGGESTED NEXT STEPS with the ! ▸ ✓ ⓘ legend and "No score, on purpose".
3. ABSORB NEXT and ITEM ON/OFF ("Compare, don't cover").

**First 30 seconds rewrite.** Open on the floor plan (not the text page) with a card: "Build a model of your room in 5 steps: size → speakers → where you sit → panels → your report. Every number is tagged: CALCULATED (exact), ESTIMATED (likely), MEASURED (yours)." Show one line of hearing safety with a link. The first instruction is "Drag the corners to match your room's size", and the Review page opens on TRY FIRST.
