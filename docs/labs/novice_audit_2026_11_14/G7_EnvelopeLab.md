## Module 1 · The Envelope Explorer

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| First look | Big white sentence "Every sound has a shape in time. Move the sliders and watch the envelope…", then a green/yellow arrow-like shape on a chart marked A D S R. Thinks: "the shape of a sound — OK". |
| Overwhelm | 2 — clean. Five sliders (Attack, Decay, Sustain level, Release, Hold) plus two curve toggles and RESET; readout and THE WORDS below. |
| Knows what it is learning? | Yes. Term card: "The envelope is the shape of a sound's loudness from start to finish. A drum hit has a spiky envelope… A bowed string has a gentle one that swells and holds." — perfect beginner wording. |
| Knows what to do first? | Yes: "▸ Try it: drag ATTACK all the way left, then all the way right." Dragging Attack to 1995 ms visibly stretches the rise across most of the chart, the A label slides, the gold dots move apart and the "rise 10→90 %" tag updates. Immediate, clear feedback. |
| Words they would not know | ms (milliseconds — never spelled out), rise time 10→90 % (explained in the readout: "skips the first and last 10 % of the climb" — good), LINEAR (curve toggle), "×3.8 slower than real time", "Hold (energy still supplied)", ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL. |
| Follows / continues? | Yes. THE WORDS · THE FOUR STAGES defines each stage plainly. "NEXT: COMMON SOUND SHAPES ›" at the end. |
| What works | Term card; "Try it" line; chart labels A/D/S/R under each region; readout sentence that interprets the setting ("A medium attack — the onset is audible as a shape, not a click"). |
| Suggestions | 1. (S) Spell out "ms = thousandths of a second" once, next to the first slider. 2. (S) The "waveform inside it" (promised in the header) is drawn as a filled colour band; a beginner sees a coloured blob, not a wave. A caption "the wiggles inside are the sound wave itself" or a thinner wave line would make the promise visible. 3. (S) Badge "ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL — DRAWN FROM THE SETTINGS, NOT A MEASUREMENT" → "A drawing of your settings, not a recording" (SHARED wording issue F5). |
| Hobbyist | Knows ADSR from synths; the rise-time 10→90 % idea is new and useful. |

| Suggestions | 1. (M) Add a plain caption under the chart: "Big gap = a spiky sound (drums). Small gap = a steady sound (trumpet)." before any dB numbers. 2. (S) Define dB in one bracket the first time ("dB — a step of loudness"). |

## Lab verdict — EnvelopeLab

**Beginner's journey:** The friendliest lab in my group so far. A clear term card, then a single chart with A/D/S/R written under each part, and a "Try it" line telling them exactly which slider to drag. Real instruments in module 2 make it concrete, and each module ends by naming the next. It gets denser at Peak vs Average (dB, RMS, VU, gain structure), and it ends with two near-identical quizzes in a row.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. Module 5's numbers in dB arrive with no plain-words caption.
2. Two back-to-back quizzes with near-identical names and overlapping questions.
3. Small things that blur feedback: each shape fills the same width whatever its length; the "GOT IT ›" on module 6 vanishes without saying what it did.

**3 best things**
1. "▸ Try it:" first-step line on every module.
2. MODULE n / 8 ▾ list + "NEXT: <next module name> ›".
3. Common Sound Shapes with real instruments.

**First 30 seconds rewrite:** Already close. Keep the term card; skip the intro popup and let the "Try it" line carry it: "Drag ATTACK left — the sound starts with a snap. Drag it right — it fades in slowly. That rise is the 'attack'." Add "ms = thousandths of a second" under the first slider.
