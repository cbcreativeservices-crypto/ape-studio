# G3 · ConnectorSelectLab (Audio Connectors & Cable Selection): beginner orientation audit

Walked 2026-11-14 at 390 x 844, sound off, tab-7, `http://localhost:8142/#labpreview/ConnectorSelectLab` (needs a full page load; a hash change from another lab keeps the old lab mounted). 16 module positions in 7 "stations"; a strip under the title (‹ PREV · MODULE n / 16 ▾ · NEXT ›) and a green NEXT: <PAGE> › at the bottom. Each page title sits in the header ("Connector, cable, signal") with the lab name small under it, which orients better than CableLab's fixed header.

Shared things met on first open:
- Open order: glossary card "Connector" first, then the screen-guide card (WHAT YOU'RE LEARNING / WHERE TO START), which on page 1 appeared a moment AFTER the page was usable, so my first tap on a cable part was interrupted by it. (SHARED)
- **The glossary card contradicts the lab.** The "Connector" card says: "Each family fits its own jack and carries its own kind of signal. The right connector matches the signal and the job." Seconds later the lab's CENTRAL LESSON says: "A connector's shape does not determine the signal, cable construction, level, or protocol." A beginner's very first fact is the one the lab spends 16 pages un-teaching. (glossary text, SHARED source)
- Each page ends with small goal pills ("○ Tap all 8 cable parts", "○ Open all 4 questions"): a clear, low-stress definition of done. Very good.
- This lab covers much of the same ground as CableLab (exploded cable, TRS two jobs, look-alikes, tester, safety). A beginner who has done CableLab will feel déjà vu; one who has not will not know which to do first. Neither lab says how it relates to the other.

## ConnectorSelectLab: overall verdict
This is the friendlier of the two cable labs. Each page has its own title in the header, a short opening line, small "○ goal" pills that define done, and cards that stay closed until tapped, so the first screen of most pages is calm. Its signature (the four questions FIT · SIGNAL · CONSTRUCTION · SAFETY, set on page 1 and then used as the verdict rows when a cable choice is wrong) is the best learning device in either cable lab: a wrong answer teaches which step of reasoning failed. The beginner's journey stumbles on vocabulary used before it is explained ("balanced" from page 1, explained on page 8; "protocol"), on a glossary card that says the opposite of the lab's central lesson, on page 6's silent red marks, and on long stacked tester pages. The pro scenarios on page 7 come before the home ones on page 11, which is the wrong way round for confidence.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. The "Connector" glossary card at first open teaches the misconception the lab exists to remove ("Each family … carries its own kind of signal").
2. Key words used before they are explained: balanced (explained on page 8), protocol (half-explained on page 5), level, phantom power, DI.
3. Feedback gaps: page 6 shows red marks with no words on a wrong attempt; page 15's answers are all FALSE (and the guide says so); pages 12, 13 and 16 stack 4, 5 and 12 items on one scroll.

**3 best things**
1. The four questions as a reusable habit AND as the scoring frame for wrong picks (page 9).
2. Real photos next to drawings (page 1 stripped cable, page 8 cut end, page 6 male/female/cable).
3. The tester's LAMP and the diagnosis lock ("A tech tests before deciding"), plus the everyday traps (charge-only USB-C, headset TRRS, TV ARC).

**First 30 seconds, rewritten**
No glossary card first. Show the stripped-cable photo with: "Three things are in your hand: the plug you see, the cable you can't, and the signal that isn't in either. This lab trains one habit: before you plug in, ask four questions — does it fit, what signal, what cable, is it safe?" Then the first tap: one part of the cable. Say in one line that this is the "choosing" lab and Cable & Connector Fundamentals is the "naming" lab, and that either can come first.

Source check (after the walk): `pagesB.tsx` confirms job explanations render only when every mark is right (`checked && allRight`). `data/jobs.ts` holds a good one-line `explain` for every job, so the words for suggestion 6.1 already exist.
