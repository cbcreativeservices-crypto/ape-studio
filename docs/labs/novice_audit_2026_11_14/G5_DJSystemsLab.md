## Screens 6-14 (read in full; condensed to the fields that differ)

Format per screen: **First look · Overwhelm (1-5) · Knows what / what to do · Words · What works · Suggestions (size)**.

**Screen 6 · Set gain and read meters.** First look: mixer top view (two input selectors, TRIM 1/2, four meters, BUS OVER light); readouts CH1 AVG, CH2 AVG, MASTER, MIX BUS. Overwhelm 4. Knows what: partly: the intro's target is numeric ("ride TRIM 1 until CH1 AVG sits between −21 and −15 dBFS with PK under −3"). Words: trim, gain, average, peak, headroom, dBFS, mix bus, "after TRIM, EQ and FILTER, before the fader". Works: the idea "set TRIM once per track, so the fader is left for the mix" is clear and practical; "clipping can happen at the mix bus even when each channel is clean". Suggestions: 1. Give the target as a coloured zone on the meter ("keep it in the green box") rather than only dBFS numbers. M. 2. Gloss dBFS at first use ("0 = the loudest the mixer can carry; below 0 is safe"). S.

**Screen 7 · Headphone cue and booth monitoring.** First look: part of the mixer with CUE 1 / CUE 2, SPLIT/BLEND, PHONES, a pair of headphones drawn as worn, and a booth monitor. Overwhelm 3. Knows what: yes ("The crowd hears the MASTER; you hear the next track first, privately"). Words: PFL, cue/master blend, split cue, booth level. Works: the headphone cups light amber for cue and blue for master "from what that ear really carries": a strong silent picture of an audio idea. Suggestions: 1. Put the one-line idea (crowd hears master, you hear cue) as a caption on the drawing. S.

**Screen 8 · Channel faders and crossfaders.** First look: faders, crossfader, assign and curve switches under a crossfader curve plot. Overwhelm 3-4. Words: crossfader, curve, BLEND/DIP/CUT, "each side −3 dB in the middle", reverse (hamster). Works: the live curve plot; the surprising fact "two identical copies add up louder than two different tracks". Suggestions: 1. Explain the three curves in plain words first ("smooth swap · dip in the middle · instant switch for scratching") before the dB values. S. 2. Gloss "hamster" ("reversed crossfader, a scratch-DJ setting"). S.

**Screen 9 · EQ isolators and filters.** First look: EQ and filter knobs for both channels over a frequency-response plot; "The ring marks the knob you are moving." Overwhelm 3. Knows what: yes ("why two bass lines playing together blur"). Words: EQ band, isolator, filter, resonance, Hz, kHz, KILL. Works: "turn LOW 2 down to KILL. Watch the plot: the low end falls away" (immediate visual feedback); the bass-handover idea with two keys side by side. Suggestions: 1. Name the bands in plain words on the plot ("LOW = bass and kick · MID = voices · HIGH = hats and shine") instead of only 250 Hz / 2.5 kHz. S.

**Screen 10 · Tempo, beatmatching and phrasing.** First look: two zoomed waveforms with beat grids and a phase strip; readouts A BPM 122.00, B BPM 126.00, B vs A, A PHRASE 1/8. Overwhelm 4 (two problems that sound alike: drift vs offset; split cue in the left ear; starting on a phrase). Knows what to do: yes, but timing-dependent ("press PLAY B when A PHRASE turns 1 / 8"). Without sound, beatmatching by ear cannot be practised; the phase strip carries it. Words: BPM, phrase, drift, offset, split cue, nudge. Works: naming DRIFT vs OFFSET as two different problems is excellent teaching. Suggestions: 1. One picture per problem: "DRIFT = the beats slide apart over time · OFFSET = the beats are apart from the start" with mini animations. M. 2. Give a wider timing window or a "start B on the next phrase for me" helper for the first try. S.

**Screen 11 · Beatgrids, sync and quantize.** First look: two waveforms with grid lines (white beats, amber bars) and violet kicks; SYNC and QUANTIZE keys. Overwhelm 3. Knows what: yes, and the key insight is strong: "SYNC and QUANTIZE follow the beatgrid, not the music". Words: beatgrid, first beat, half/double BPM, sync, quantize. Works: the experiment where SYNC matches tempos but kicks still land apart (because the grid is wrong) teaches by surprise. Suggestions: 1. Say in step 1 that "the software guesses where the beats are; if it guesses wrong, SYNC is wrong too" before the vocabulary. S.

**Screen 12 · Cue points, loops and samples.** First look: deck B's waveform with cue points, two small meters (your headphones · the crowd), hot cue pads, loop and jump keys. Overwhelm 3. Words: hot cue, loop, beat jump, sampler, quantize, transport CUE. Works: two meters that show whether a sample reaches the crowd or only your headphones (makes "private vs public" visible); the fault title "The sample hit the dance floor". Suggestions: 1. Label the two meters in words on the drawing ("YOU HEAR" / "CROWD HEARS"), if not already. S.

**Screen 13 · Effects and their routing.** First look: an FX SCOPE (white dry line in, blue effect band out) over a map of where the effect sits. Overwhelm 4 (insert, master, send/return, dry/wet, feedback, effect time in beats and ms). Works: the scope ("each repeat is a step of the blue band, one delay apart") lets a silent learner see an echo. Suggestions: 1. Teach one placement first (on one channel), then "MORE: on the master / on a send" as a second step. S. 2. Gloss "dry/wet" ("dry = original, wet = effect"). S.

**Screen 14 · Playback speed, pitch and key.** First look: a pitch-vs-tempo plot, a 12-semitone strip with each deck's key, deck B's tempo column. Overwhelm 4. Words: semitone, key, key lock, key shift, artifacts, ±8% range. Works: a simple rule ("about one semitone for every 6 % of tempo") and a one-tap demonstration (MATCH TEMPO TO A: "deck B slows 3.2 % … key marker slip below D"). Suggestions: 1. For non-musicians, explain "key" and "semitone" in one line ("a semitone is one piano key up or down; two tracks in clashing keys sound sour"). S.

Hobbyist (screens 6-14): this is the heart of DJ craft and well taught; a bedroom DJ will learn the why behind SYNC, KEY LOCK and gain staging. The dBFS targets and dB curve values are fine for them.

## Screens 15-24 (read in full; condensed)

**Screen 15 · Stems and source separation.** First look: deck A's four stems as separate waveforms (drums, bass, chords, melody) under one playhead; muted parts hatched. Overwhelm 2-3. Knows what: yes; the picture IS the idea. Words: stem, original vs separated, leakage, "the drop" (glossed: "the busiest, fullest part"), "the hook" (glossed). Works: in-line glosses of DJ slang in brackets: a model for the whole lab. Suggestions: 1. Show leakage visually (a faint ghost of the drums inside the separated bass lane). M.

**Screen 16 · Laptop software and audio interfaces (marked "An optional screen, for a laptop rig").** First look: the software's AUDIO SETUP window above controller · laptop · mixer. Overwhelm 3. Words: audio interface, buffer, latency, dropout, output device. Works: the "optional" label is honest and lowers pressure; the opening task is a real first-gig fault (output device set to BUILT-IN, so nothing reaches the crowd). Suggestions: 1. Explain buffer with a picture ("music is sent in small buckets: bigger buckets = fewer gaps, more delay"). S.

**Screen 17 · Vinyl turntables and phono audio (optional, for turntables).** First look: tonearm from the side above the turntable from the top; ARM, PHONO and ROOM views. Overwhelm 3-4. Words: tracking force, anti-skate, cartridge, phono stage, ground wire, stylus. Works: the "dial says 4.0 g, the scale says 2.5 g" discovery (trust measurement, not the dial). Suggestions: 1. One-line purpose up top: "Three things make vinyl sound right: weight on the needle, one phono stage, a ground wire." S.

**Screen 18 · Digital vinyl systems (optional).** First look: a control-signal ring, a signal map, the turntable with a control record, rear cables. Overwhelm 4 for a non-DJ (the whole concept is unusual). Works: "the ring collapses to a dot, and the music stops": a clear visible cause and effect. Words: control record, calibrate, absolute/relative/internal. Suggestions: 1. Start with the plain idea: "The record plays no music, only a code; the laptop listens to the code and plays the music to match your hand." S.

**Screen 19 · Microphones, announcements and talkover.** First look: the mixer front panel with two mic inputs, a mic, four meters (MIC, CROWD, BOOTH, REC). Overwhelm 3. Words: phantom power (+48 V), talkover (ducking), feedback, mic send, dBFS target "−18 to −8". Works: honest "check the manual of the one you use"; the fault "The recording has no voice" (ties to screen 21). Suggestions: 1. Give the gain target as a zone on the MIC meter. S.

**Screen 20 · PA speakers, subs and venue connections.** First look: the back of the mixer and a venue stage box 20 m away, inputs labelled DJ L / DJ R (13, 14). Overwhelm 3-4. Knows what: yes; "your MASTER is a line feed to someone else's system" is a key professional idea. Works: the role-play ("ask the engineer for more ('UP 3 dB')"). Words: house left, balanced line, unity, crossover, coverage angle. Suggestions: 1. Say why balanced cables matter in one plain line at first use ("long cables pick up hum unless they are balanced"). S.

**Screen 21 · Recording and streaming.** First look: back of the mixer and a handheld recorder with nothing in LINE IN. Overwhelm 3. Works: "only playback proves it"; the experiment "ride MASTER: the room changes, the RECORDER's meters do not" (separate tap, separate level). Words: record tap, capture level, loopback, monitoring loop. Suggestions: 1. Draw the three taps (REC OUT / BOOTH OUT / MASTER) as three labelled arrows to the recorder. S.

**Screen 22 · Hybrid DJ systems.** First look: mixer back (MIDI OUT, CLOCK switch), drum machine back and top with step lights. Overwhelm 4. Knows what: yes; "A clock cable carries no sound, and an audio cable carries no tempo." is excellent. Words: MIDI, clock, 5-pin, TRS-MIDI, external sync. Suggestions: 1. Colour the two cable kinds differently in the drawing (sound vs timing) and say so in a caption. S.

**Screen 23 · Performer changeovers and backup playback.** First look: a signal-flow board (laptops and players → mixer strips and master → speakers and headphones; power cords to the booth strip or a wall socket "on another circuit"). Overwhelm 4. Works: PREDICT ("which disconnection would stop the music right now? Check your answer"): predict-then-check again. Words: input list, USB audio, backup playback, ground loop. Suggestions: 1. Define ground loop in plain words where the wall-socket hint appears ("two power circuits can make a hum"). S.

**Screen 24 · Troubleshooting and the complete system challenge (FINISH).** First look: the full event board with every device, its meter and its power cord; readouts EVENT #6483 · SETTING UP, POWER 0/7, THE ROOM, PLAYING · STOPPED. Overwhelm 4 (the whole job), but it is the capstone. Knows what to do: yes: a 6-goal PRACTICE list in plain verbs ("Power up safely: speakers last · Cue deck B privately · A clean transition: no gap, no clip · Announce to the room AND the recording…"). Works: "find it from the evidence, not by guessing"; a debrief step. Suggestions: 1. Offer SHOW A HINT per goal as the Game Audio capstone does. S. 2. The event number "#6483" means nothing to a beginner; drop it or explain it ("each run has its own fault"). S.

## DJ SYSTEMS LAB · overall verdict

**The beginner's journey.** A newcomer sees a beautiful, realistic booth and quickly grasps "two decks and a mixer". Screen 2's "four jobs" (play, mix, you hear, room hears) is a superb organiser, and many screens teach by surprise (SYNC matches but kicks still clash; the dial says 4 g, the scale says 2.5 g; the room changes but the recording does not). The weight comes from three things: every screen carries a second job (a TROUBLESHOOT fault) from screen 1; step-1 paragraphs often spend 60-90 words describing the drawing's geography (mirrored views, scales, which way left is) before the idea; and numeric targets in dBFS and dB curves are given where a coloured zone would do. Optional screens (16-18) are honestly labelled as optional, and DJ slang is often glossed in brackets, which a beginner will appreciate.

**3 biggest orientation problems**
1. **Two jobs per screen from the very first one:** a lesson and a "Something will go wrong in the booth" fault on every screen, including the welcome screen; plus an exposure calculator with NIOSH/OSHA on screen 1.
2. **Long "You are looking at…" geography paragraphs** (screens 3, 4, 7, 13, 23, 24) that describe the drawing in detail before the one idea of the screen; mirrored back-of-gear views (3, 4, 20, 22) add a left/right puzzle.
3. **First-step mismatches and number targets:** the intro card and step 2 often name different first controls (screens 1, 2, 3); targets as dBFS ranges (screens 6, 19) and dB curve values (8) rather than visual zones; "FIND 1/4" style counters with no label.

**3 best things**
1. **The booth drawing and its listening tiles** (CROWD · BOOTH · PHONES), with headphone cups that light amber for cue and blue for master: the lab teaches audio by sight, which works with sound off.
2. **Ideas that land in one sentence:** "four jobs", "the crowd hears the MASTER; you hear the next track first, privately", "SYNC follows the grid, not the music", "a clock cable carries no sound, and an audio cable carries no tempo", "your MASTER is a line feed to someone else's system".
3. **Real-world faults and predictions:** "The sample hit the dance floor", "A hum on the dance floor", PREDICT on screen 23, a full event capstone with a debrief.

**"First 30 seconds" rewrite**
Screen 1 caption over the booth: "Every DJ setup does four jobs: PLAY the music, MIX it, let YOU hear the next track, let the ROOM hear the mix. You will wire, play and fix this booth, one screen at a time." Then one step: "Press ■ STOP once so you know where it is, then PLAY A." Move the NIOSH/OSHA exposure tile to the end of screen 1 (or its own short screen), and start the TROUBLESHOOT cards from screen 3, after the learner knows what "working" looks like.
