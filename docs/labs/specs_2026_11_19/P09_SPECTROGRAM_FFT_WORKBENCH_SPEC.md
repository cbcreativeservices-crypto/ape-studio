## 1. Summary for the owner

**What the learner does.** They build a spectrogram themselves, one setting at a time, and find out when the picture is telling the truth and when it is not. They pick a block of samples and watch it turn into frequency bins. They change the window shape and watch a quiet tone appear from under a loud one. They try to separate two whistles 20 Hz apart, then try to pin a drum hit to within 5 ms, and find that no single setting does both. Then they read real recordings (speech, guitar, a passing car, hum, a click, a room tail), spot three "traps" where the feature is made by the display and not the sound, slow down an earthquake and a bat call so they can be heard, and look at bird, whale and frog choruses. Every picture is computed from the real signal the learner can hear. Nothing is drawn by hand.

**Shape and size.** Six modules, 29 screens (one of them optional), each module ending in a three-question check. The lab extends Visual Audio Analysis: that lab teaches how to *read* a spectrogram, and this one teaches how the picture is *made*. **Size: L**, larger than Comp B's "M". Comp B assumed the app's FFT engine could be reused. It cannot: the native engine runs one fixed window (Hann) on the microphone only, with no way to choose the window, zero-padding or overlap. So this lab needs a new JavaScript analysis core (section 4). That core is plain, testable code, and **no new native build is needed**. The whole lab can go out by over-the-air update.

**Access.** It sits in the Training section under a new category, **Measurement & Analysis** (H19 default). **Module 1 is a free taster** (H22). Modules 2–6 are members-only through the house `useMemberGate()`.

**Credit.** One whole-lab key, `tr_spectrogram`, plus six module keys (`spg_…`). The module keys are needed because the owner kept Comp B's module subsets (H18 default): several credentials require only modules 1–4, and some of those add module 5 or 6. Credit is never removed.

**What it needs from you (details in section 8):**
1. Your **go** to build (D56).
2. Approval of the **recording list and licences** for modules 4–6, about 15 clips. Because the app is commercial, only public-domain, CC0 or CC BY material qualifies; no "NonCommercial" or "NoDerivatives" licences. The credits go on an "audio credits" screen behind the ⓘ (H14/H15). Also: would you rather record the speech and guitar clips yourself? Modules 1–3 do not wait on this, because every sound in them is generated by the app.
3. Permission to make the **Labs-menu tile image**, under the image rule. This blocks the listing, not the build.

## 2. Learning outcomes (knowledge, never skills)

After the lab, the learner:

1. **Understands** how an FFT turns one block of samples into frequency bins, and why the bin spacing is the sample rate divided by the FFT size (Δf = fs / N) while the block lasts N / fs seconds.
2. **Understands** the time-versus-frequency trade-off: a short window shows *when* something happened, and a long window shows *which frequency* it was. They know why no single setting does both.
3. **Knows** what spectral leakage is (a tone that does not fit the block whole spreads into neighbouring bins), and that window shape trades a narrow main lobe for low side lobes, with the Kaiser β setting moving along that trade.
4. **Knows** that zero-padding gives a smoother-looking curve but does not separate two close frequencies. Only a longer stretch of real signal does that.
5. **Understands** that overlap, colour range, floor and axis scale change how a spectrogram *looks* without changing the sound. They also know that brighter colour means more level in the file, not "louder as heard".
6. **Knows** the common features on a spectrogram (harmonics, formants, transients, hum lines, clicks, Doppler sweeps, reverberation tails) and three common display artefacts (leakage skirts, time smear, a tone hidden below the colour floor).
7. **Knows** how very slow signals (seismic, infrasound) and very fast ones (bat calls, ultrasound) are brought into view and into hearing: speeding up, slowing down, and choosing a sample rate.
8. **Knows** why soundscape indices and species "frequency bands" are ideas to use with care, because the number changes with the analysis settings.

Things the lab must never teach (Comp B, kept): that a spectrogram *is* the sound; that more FFT points are always better; that colour intensity is loudness as heard.

## 3. Screen-by-screen plan

### Layout common to every screen

- **Rack Unit** (`src/screens/lab/rack/RackUnit.tsx`). The **display** is pinned at the top: the spectrogram, time across and frequency up, coloured by the house heat scale (`heatColor` in `features/tools/levelColor`) with a level legend. Under it, on screens that use them, sit the single-frame **spectrum at the cursor** and the **window shape**. The **well** scrolls in between and holds the prose. **Controls are docked at the bottom** (ParamLane plus DockButtons). The **bezel** carries read-only readouts through `BezelReadouts`: FFT SIZE, BIN WIDTH (Hz), WINDOW (ms), HOP (ms), CURSOR f / t / level. A cropped readout drops its label, never its number (D36).
- **Level labels always say what they are.** The legend reads "dBFS — the level in the file, not how loud it sounds". The lab never shows a bare "dB" and never uses dBFS without saying so.
- **Colour is never the only cue.** Tapping the display prints the exact level, frequency and time at that point. The spectrum slice under the cursor shows the same column as a curve, and a contour toggle (one dB step) is available where colour detail matters (charter §8).
- **Every display has its audio.** A PLAY key in the dock plays it through the `earPlayer` pipeline under `startFenced`. A playhead runs across the spectrogram, driven per animation frame on the UI thread.
- **Readouts update every animation frame** from shared values (charter §7). There is no per-frame React state.
- **Controls follow the house fader rules (D75).** Three or four positions are a tap key (`kind: 'options'`): window type, overlap, dynamic range, linear/log axis, sample rate. Longer ranges use a lane: FFT size in power-of-two steps; tone frequency on a log taper; Kaiser β from 0 to 14. A control that has no effect on a screen is `locked: '<reason>'`, never silently dead.
- **Each screen has** a ScreenGuide written in the student's voice (intro: learning ≤ 30 words, start ≤ 40 words; then seeing / control / changes / teaches / tryThis), an ⓘ, and an accessible Canvas label with a live text summary.
- **Display text is at least 9 pt at 390 px wide** after `fitValue`.
- **Navigation never blocks.** NEXT always moves on, and a hands-on page that is not done is listed on the what's-left screen, not walled off. **Each module ends on the shared what's-left screen** (`kit/LabEndScreen` through PagedLab). The hub has its own what's-left summary of the six modules.
- **Settings are suggested starting points, never rules.** The lab says "a good place to start is…" and avoids "always" and "never", except for hearing safety.

### Hub: `SpectrogramLab` (open to everyone)

- Six module rows with progress: done, in progress, or not started. The row list has three faces: loading, UNREADABLE (`ProgressUnreadableNote`) and fresh. A failed read is never shown as "nothing done".
- Module 1 opens for everyone. Modules 2–6 follow `useMemberGate()`:
  - **open**: the module opens;
  - **locked**: a 🔒 and the membership offer, and only where `useUpsellAllowed()` permits;
  - **checking**: "Checking your account…";
  - **unconfirmed**: `MEMBERSHIP_NOT_CONFIRMED`.
- The ⓘ opens an "About this lab" note (one short note on starting points, H14) and the **Audio credits** screen.
- A "Read it first?" link goes to Visual Audio Analysis, module 6 (`MeterModule {id:'spectrogram'}`). It is a suggestion, never a lock.

## 4. Audio and DSP

**What exists today, checked in the code:**

- **Native engine** (`modules/ape-dsp`, `ios/core/Fft.hpp`, `EngineHub.hpp`): a radix-2 FFT with a **fixed Hann window**, size 256–16384. It runs only on the **microphone ring**, and its fine spectrum is the averaged `getSpectrum()`. There is no window choice, no zero-padding, no overlap control, and no raw-sample export to JavaScript. It cannot analyse an arbitrary file or a generated buffer. Only the optional live page 3.5 uses it.
- **JavaScript:**
  - `src/features/ear/earDsp.ts` has the signal generators (sine, noise, harmonic complex, reverb, biquads), `encodeWav`, and a Hann-only `powerSpectrumDb`. That function is not calibrated to dBFS: it divides by N², so a full-scale sine reads about −12 dB.
  - `src/features/ear/earPlayer.ts` plays rendered buffers (Float32 → WAV → cache file → expo-audio) with the output ceiling.
  - `src/features/tools/measure/spectrogramRaster.ts` builds Skia raster images from dB grids. Its axis is fixed at 50 Hz–16 kHz and 128 rows, and it is tied to saved snapshots, so it is not changed. The lab reuses its `heatColor` ramp and its image-building technique.
- **Visual Audio Analysis** draws its spectrogram from a deterministic *teaching model* (`meterEngine`, "synthetic patterns"). P09 must not reuse those patterns, because the owner's rule is real computed data.

**The plan: a JavaScript STFT core.** The new pure module is `src/features/dsp/stft.ts`, with no React Native imports so Node can test it. It provides:

- a real-input FFT (an N/2 complex transform plus a split step, with precomputed twiddles and bit-reverse tables per size);
- the windows: rectangular, Hann, Blackman and Kaiser, with Kaiser computed from a series for the Bessel function I₀;
- zero-padding, hop and overlap, and dB conversion normalised by the window's sum (coherent gain), so a bin-centred full-scale sine reads 0 dBFS;
- **window metrics computed from the window itself**: main-lobe width, highest side lobe, coherent gain, equivalent noise bandwidth and scalloping loss. These are taken from a heavily zero-padded FFT of the window, so the bezel numbers are calculated, never stored.

**Is JavaScript fast enough on a phone? Probably, but NOT MEASURED.**

- Work estimate: a real FFT of N = 4096 is about 2 × 2048 × 11 ≈ 45,000 complex multiply-adds.
- A 5 s clip at 48 kHz, N = 2048 and 75 % overlap is about 470 frames, so about 5 million multiply-adds per recompute.
- Hermes has no optimising compiler for typed-array loops, so the time on a mid-range phone is **UNKNOWN**. A plausible range is a few hundred milliseconds per full recompute, which is too slow for a single block on the JS thread but fine when spread out.

The design copes either way:

1. **Phase 0 performance proof first.** Before any screen is built, run a dev-only frame-time probe on the owner's Pixel (charter §7, side-by-side dev build, rule 33) timing `stft.ts` at the lab's largest settings. iOS is reported as NOT MEASURED.
2. **Never block a frame.** A recompute is cut into chunks of columns with `runSoon()`, and the spectrogram fills left to right as columns arrive, which also shows how it is made. Results are cached per (clip, settings).
3. **Single-frame screens are cheap.** Modules 1 and 2 compute one FFT per change and stay live while a lane is dragged. A drag that outruns the compute shows the last finished frame, never an invented one.
4. **Long records** (the module 5 seismograms, which run to minutes or hours) are pre-computed at build time by a Node script that calls **the same `stft.ts`** on the real data, at a small set of settings. The results are stored as compact dB grids, not bitmaps, and drawn by the same raster code. That keeps them real computed data, as Comp B allowed.
5. **Cursor and slice:** the column data goes to the UI thread once per recompute, and the cursor, slice and bezel read it per frame in worklets.

**Does anything need a NATIVE build? No.** Everything above uses what is already in the store binaries:

- Skia 2.6.2, Reanimated 4.5, Gesture Handler ~2.32 (all on the `final-lab` line);
- expo-audio, expo-asset, expo-file-system (`legacy`), react-native-webview (not needed here);
- the ApeDsp engine v2+ for the optional page 3.5.

New `.wav` assets ship inside an over-the-air update: assets are not part of the native runtime fingerprint, and Metro's default asset list includes `wav`. To be exact, a native build would be needed **only** if the lab were changed to do one of these, and none is in this spec:

- (a) apply the lab's own windows, overlap or zero-padding to the **live microphone**: the engine would need raw-sample export or new window code in `modules/` (D41: a `modules/` change goes to the native build queue);
- (b) play files at unusual sample rates natively. The plan avoids this by resampling audified and time-expanded audio to 48 kHz in JS before `encodeWav`, because device behaviour for odd-rate WAVs is UNKNOWN.

**Signal sources.**

- **Generated (modules 1–3, plus hum, click and room tail in module 4):** tones, close tone pairs, chirps, a synthetic drum hit, noise and clicks, from `earDsp` generators or `stft.ts` helpers at a stated sample rate. Each is deterministic, so a goal can be checked against the known truth.
- **Recorded (modules 4–6, about 15 clips):**
  - speech, guitar and car pass-by: CC0 or CC BY, or recorded by the owner;
  - seismogram(s) and infrasound: IRIS/EarthScope open data, H15;
  - whale: NOAA, US government work;
  - bat call: CC0 or CC BY high-sample-rate recording;
  - birds and frog chorus: CC0 or CC BY.
  - **Licence rule:** public domain, CC0 or CC BY only. NC and ND are excluded because the app is commercial.
  - Every asset gets a row in `docs/labs/spectrogram/ASSET_LICENCES.md` and an entry on the Audio credits screen.
- **Storage:** 16-bit mono WAV at the source's own sample rate, decoded in JS by a small WAV parser, with an asset budget of about 8 MB in the update. If the chosen set exceeds that, the fallback is download-on-first-open, which needs Comp A storage work and is not proposed by default.

**Safe level (H08 default: cap and warn).**

- All playback goes through `earPlayer` and `outputCeiling`: **−12 dBFS ceiling, no unlock**.
- Every clip is loudness-matched (`normalizeRms`, then a peak check), so switching clips never jumps in level.
- Short fades (`fadeEdges`) on every start and stop prevent clicks.
- A safe-listening notice appears before the first playback each session, in the DJ Systems Lab's pattern ("start low, keep STOP in reach"), and only after the app-wide audio gate allows sound.
- Ultrasonic and infrasonic content is never played at real speed; only the time-expanded or sped-up versions are, high-passed at 20 Hz after the shift.
- Every start goes through `startFenced`, and sound stops on blur (`useStopOnBlur`).

**Understanding-check ideas.** Three per module, exactly one correct option matched by value, an explanation for every option, no brand names. Any graded choice accepts every reasonable answer.

- **M1:**
  - (a) At 48 kHz, which FFT size gives bins about 11.7 Hz wide? (4096)
  - (b) Two tones 20 Hz apart show as one hump. What separates them? (a longer block)
  - (c) What does a bigger FFT size cost? (the block lasts longer, so timing blurs)
- **M2:**
  - (a) Why does a pure tone spread into its neighbours? (it does not fit the block a whole number of times)
  - (b) A weak tone hides beside a strong one. Which change helps? (a window with lower side lobes)
  - (c) Padding with zeros makes the curve smoother. Does it separate two close tones? (no)
- **M3:**
  - (a) What does more overlap change? (how densely columns are drawn, not resolution)
  - (b) Raising the colour floor makes a tone vanish. Is it gone from the sound? (no)
  - (c) Why can't one setting both separate the whistles and time the hit to 5 ms? (a long window blurs time; a short one blurs frequency)
- **M4:**
  - (a) Smear before a drum hit on a long window: in the sound or the display? (display)
  - (b) A thin line at 60 Hz with lines at 120 and 180 Hz is most likely what? (mains hum and its harmonics)
  - (c) How exact can a frequency reading be? (about half a bin at these settings)
- **M5:**
  - (a) Speeding up 500 times turns 2 Hz into what? (1 kHz)
  - (b) Why record a bat at a very high sample rate? (to capture frequencies above half the usual rate)
  - (c) Slowing down 10 times turns 45 kHz into what? (4.5 kHz)
- **M6:**
  - (a) Why did the index change when only the FFT size changed? (the index depends on analysis settings)
  - (b) Birdsong trills blur. Which window length helps? (shorter)
  - (c) Do species always keep to separate bands? (no; it is one idea to test)
