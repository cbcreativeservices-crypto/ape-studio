# Topic Foundations — grading rubric (2026-11-03)

Each lesson is graded on **8 categories**, each scored **1 to 5**. The categories come straight from the owner-approved standard (`TOPIC_FOUNDATIONS_STANDARD.md`). Graders are independent: they did not write or fix the lessons.

## The scale (same for every category)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **5** | Excellent. Fully meets the standard; nothing worth changing. Matches the five owner-approved lessons. |
| **4** | Good. Meets the standard; one or two small things could be better, none that would mislead or confuse. |
| **3** | Acceptable. Meets the standard in the main, but has a noticeable weakness a reader would feel. Should be fixed before approval. |
| **2** | Weak. Fails part of the standard in a way that misleads, confuses or leaves out a large part. Must be fixed. |
| **1** | Failing. Wrong, unsafe, or does not do the job at all. Must be rewritten. |

## The 8 categories

| # | Category | Weight | What a 5 looks like | What pulls it down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accuracy** | ×3 | Every statement is true; simplifications never become false; exceptions handled with "often", "usually"; safety advice is correct and complete | A false or misleading claim; an absolute ("never", "only") that is not true; unsafe or incomplete safety advice |
| 2 | **Scope** | ×2 | Introduces the WHOLE topic as its title and term list define it; every large term group (about 10%+) is in the lesson in rough proportion | A large group missing; one corner of the topic taking over; content that belongs to a sibling topic |
| 3 | **Flow** | ×2 | One line of thought: each paragraph grows from the last; no side blocks, lists or second unrelated example; a term is named only when needed | A jump between ideas; a paragraph that could be moved without loss; a term used before it is understood |
| 4 | **Beginner clarity** | ×2 | A curious 16-year-old new to the topic follows every sentence on first read; detail stays in the flashcards | Too advanced; too many terms or ideas packed together; assumes knowledge the reader does not have |
| 5 | **Plain international English** | ×2 | Every sentence reads correctly when taken literally by a second-language reader; short sentences; common words | Idioms, figures of speech, personification, vague sound adjectives, unexplained abbreviations, long tangled sentences |
| 6 | **Glossary agreement** | ×1 | Every named or explained term exists in the topic's list and matches its plain-English meaning in the glossary | A term used with a different meaning from the glossary; a named term not in the topic |
| 7 | **Opening and closing** | ×1 | Opens from something the reader already knows (or a true prerequisite) and leads into the topic's question; closes by saying what the flashcards cover and what the learner will know | A weak or generic opening; an opening that repeats a sibling's scene; a closing that adds new ideas or lists |
| 8 | **House rules and form** | ×1 | No brand or company names (except the topic's own product), no citations, knowledge not skills, US spelling, 400–480 words, 6–8 paragraphs, passes the check script | Any rule broken |

## Overall grade

- **Weighted score** = (3×Accuracy + 2×Scope + 2×Flow + 2×Clarity + 2×Language + Glossary + Opening/closing + House rules) ÷ 14, rounded to one decimal.
- **Accuracy gate:** a lesson's overall score can never be more than its Accuracy score + 1. A lesson that is wrong cannot pass on style.
- **Letter grade:**

| Grade | Weighted score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | 4.5–5.0 | Ready for approval as written |
| **B** | 4.0–4.4 | Ready for approval; small polish possible |
| **C** | 3.5–3.9 | Fix the named weakness before approval |
| **D** | 3.0–3.4 | Needs a careful revision |
| **F** | below 3.0 | Rewrite |

## What a grader returns for each lesson

The 8 scores, one short reason for every score below 5, the overall comment, and — for any score of 3 or lower — the exact quote and an exact suggested fix.
