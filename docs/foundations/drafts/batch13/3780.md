Topic Foundations: Passive Components

Imagine opening a guitar amplifier. Inside is a board covered with parts: small cylinders with colored stripes, round metal cases, coils of copper wire, and tiny glass parts. They cannot add power to a signal. They only reduce, store, or direct energy. A part like this is called a passive component. What does each one do, and what makes one type better than another for a job?

The striped cylinders reduce the flow of electric current and turn some of its energy into heat. Each one is called a resistor. Each resistor has a marked value. The real value can differ from it by a small allowed amount. Each one also has a limit for the heat it can safely handle.

The round metal cases do a different job. Each one stores electric charge on two metal layers with an insulating layer between them. This part is called a capacitor. It passes changing signals, such as music, but blocks a steady voltage. Its size is measured in farads. Most audio capacitors use much smaller units, such as the microfarad and the picofarad. Many capacitors and resistors are named for the job they do in a circuit. One may pass a signal from one stage to the next, and another may keep a supply voltage steady.

The insulating layer is called the dielectric, and it decides how the part behaves. Plastic film keeps its value steady over time. An electrolytic capacitor uses a very thin oxide layer and a liquid inside, so it stores a large charge for its size. It has a plus end and a minus end, and one connected backwards can burst. With age, its liquid dries out. An old one can leak, or its flat top can swell.

A coil of wire stores energy in a different way, in a magnetic field. This part is called an inductor. It resists sudden changes in current, so it reduces high frequencies more than low ones. Its size is measured in henrys. With too much current, an iron core inside the coil cannot hold more magnetism, and the coil loses much of its effect.

The tiny glass parts let current flow in one direction only. Each one is called a diode. Diodes help change the power from a wall socket into current that flows one way only. Some diodes give off light. Others protect chips from short, sudden rises in voltage.

Other passive parts respond to the world around them. Some change their resistance with heat or light. A quartz crystal vibrates at a very steady rate, so circuits use it to keep accurate time. Some special metals are used as covers that reduce magnetic hum.

The flashcards explain each of these parts, with their units and ratings. By the end, you will know what each passive part does, how it can fail, and why a designer chooses one type.
