Topic Foundations: Passive Components

Imagine opening a guitar amplifier. Inside is a board covered with parts: cylinders with colored stripes, round cans, coils of copper wire, and tiny glass parts. They cannot add power to a signal. They only reduce, store, or direct energy. A part like this is called a passive component. What does each one do, and what makes one type better than another for a job?

The striped cylinders reduce the flow of current and turn some of its energy into heat. Each one is called a resistor. Its value is measured in ohms. Its real value can differ a little from its marked value. It also has a safe heat limit. Resistors are made from materials such as carbon, metal film, or wound wire.

The round cans store electric charge on two metal layers with an insulating layer between them. This part is called a capacitor. Its value is measured in farads, usually in small units such as the microfarad. It passes changing signals but blocks a steady voltage. Capacitors are often named for their job in a circuit, such as passing a signal from one stage to the next. Many resistors are named the same way.

The insulating layer is called the dielectric. Its material sets much of how the part behaves. Plastic film keeps its value steady over time. An electrolytic capacitor uses a very thin oxide layer, often with a liquid inside, so it stores a large charge for its size. Most electrolytic capacitors have a plus end and a minus end, and if one is connected backwards, it may burst. With age, an old one can dry out, leak or swell. Every capacitor also has a highest safe voltage, called its voltage rating.

A coil of wire stores energy in a magnetic field instead. This part is called an inductor. It resists sudden changes in current, so it reduces high frequencies more than low ones. Its value is measured in henrys. Many coils have an iron core. With too much current, the core cannot hold more magnetism, and the coil suddenly loses much of its effect.

The tiny glass parts let current flow mainly in one direction. Each one is called a diode. Most diodes contain two kinds of treated semiconductor material that meet inside. Current crosses that meeting point easily only one way. This one-way action helps turn wall power into one-way current and can protect circuits from sudden voltage rises. Different types do different jobs, and some give off light.

Other related parts react to heat, light, pressure or magnetism. A quartz crystal vibrates at a very steady rate, which circuits use to measure time.

The flashcards explain each part, with its units, ratings and the jobs it is named for. By the end, you will know what each passive part does, how it can fail, and why a designer chooses one type.
