Topic Foundations: Active Devices & Semiconductors

A microphone makes a very weak electrical signal, but a loudspeaker needs a much larger one. Making a signal larger needs a part that takes energy from a power supply. A part that needs power to work is called an active component. So how can a weak signal control a much larger current?

The most important active component is the transistor. It is usually made from specially treated silicon. A small signal at one of its three wire connections controls a much bigger current through the other two. In one main type, a small current at the control connection does this. In the other type, a voltage does it, and almost no current flows into that connection. A transistor can make a signal larger, or work as a switch that is fully on or off.

Designers connect a few transistors in standard groups called stages. Two matched transistors that share one current can compare two inputs. They amplify the difference and largely reject noise that is the same on both. That group is called a differential pair. At the output of a power amplifier, two transistors usually share the work, one for each half of the sound wave. They carry the most power and become very hot. The range of voltage and current a transistor can handle together without failing is called the safe operating area.

Many stages can be built together on one small piece of silicon, called an integrated circuit, or chip. The most common amplifier chip is the operational amplifier. It amplifies the difference between its two inputs by a very large amount. A few parts around it set exactly how much larger the signal becomes, called the gain. For audio, designers often choose types with low noise or low distortion.

Not every chip makes a signal larger. Some chips compare two voltages and show which is larger. Converter chips change a sound signal into numbers that a computer can store, and change them back again. Others select an input, delay a sound, or connect equipment to a computer.

Much of this work is digital. Digital circuits use transistors as switches with only two states, which stand for one and zero. Small groups of these switches follow fixed rules or hold one bit of memory, a single one or zero. Some memory keeps its data when the power is off. A tiny complete computer on one chip is called a microcontroller. It runs a small program, for example for the equipment's buttons and menus.
