Topic Foundations: Active Devices and Semiconductors

A microphone makes a very weak electrical signal, but a loudspeaker needs a much larger one. Making a signal larger needs a part that takes energy from a power supply. A part that uses power to control a signal is called an active component. So how can a weak signal control a much larger current?

The basic active component is the transistor. It is usually made of specially treated silicon, with three wire connections. A small signal at one of them controls a much bigger current through the other two. There are two main types. In one, a small current at the control connection does this. In the other, a voltage does it, and almost no current flows in. A transistor can make a signal larger, or work as a switch that is fully on or off.

Designers connect a few transistors into standard groups called stages. An input stage often uses two matched transistors that share one current. They compare two inputs, amplify the difference, and largely reject noise that is the same on both. That group is called a differential pair. Every transistor also adds a little noise of its own, so input stages often use low-noise types.

An output stage delivers a large current to the loudspeaker. In a power amplifier, two transistors usually share this work, one for each half of the sound wave. They often become very hot. The range of voltage and current a transistor can handle together without failing is called the safe operating area.

Many stages can be built together on one small piece of silicon, called an integrated circuit, or chip. The most common amplifier chip is the operational amplifier. It greatly amplifies the difference between its two inputs. A few parts around it set how much larger the output signal becomes. That amount is the gain. Different types of operational amplifier are made for low noise, low distortion, or a wide output range.

Not every chip makes a signal larger. Some chips compare two voltages and show which is larger. Converter chips change a sound signal into numbers that a computer can store, and back again.

Those numbers are handled by digital circuits. They use transistors as switches with only two states, which stand for one and zero. A small group of these switches can give an output that depends on its inputs by a fixed rule. Another small group can hold one bit of memory, a single one or zero. Some memory keeps its data when the power is off. A tiny complete computer on one chip is called a microcontroller. It runs a small program.

The flashcards explain each of these parts, from single transistors to complete chips. By the end, you will know how active parts let a small signal control a larger current, and how they are combined into the chips inside audio equipment.
