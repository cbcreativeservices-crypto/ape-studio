Topic Foundations: Active Devices and Semiconductors

A microphone makes a weak electrical signal, but a loudspeaker needs a much larger one. Making a signal larger needs a part that takes energy from a power supply. A part that uses power to control a signal is called an active component. So how can a weak signal control a much larger current?

The basic active component is the transistor. It is usually made of treated silicon and has three connections. A small signal at one of them controls a much bigger current through the other two. In one main type, a small current at the control connection does this. In the other, a voltage does it, and almost no current flows in. A transistor can make a signal larger, or work as a switch that is fully on or off.

Designers use one transistor, or a few together, in standard arrangements called stages. An input stage often uses two matched transistors that share one current. They compare two inputs, amplify the difference, and largely reject noise that is the same on both. That group is called a differential pair. Every transistor adds a little noise, so input stages often use low-noise types. Other stages copy a steady current, or pass a signal on to a heavier load.

An output stage delivers a large current to the loudspeaker. In many power amplifiers, two transistors or groups share this work, one for each half of the sound wave. Output stage designs are named by class, such as A, AB, or D. Output transistors often get very hot. The range of voltage and current a transistor can handle together without failing is called the safe operating area.

Many stages can be built on one small piece of silicon, called an integrated circuit, or chip. The most common amplifier chip is the operational amplifier. It greatly amplifies the difference between its two inputs. A few parts around it set how much larger the output becomes, called the gain. Some types are made for low noise, low distortion, or an output that swings almost to the supply voltage.

Not every chip makes a signal larger. Some chips compare two voltages and show which is larger. Converter chips change a sound signal into numbers, and back again.

Those numbers are handled by digital circuits. Inside them, transistors work as switches with only two states, which stand for one and zero. A small group of these switches can turn its output on only when its inputs match a fixed pattern, such as both being on. Another small group can hold one bit of memory. A complete small computer on one chip is called a microcontroller.

The flashcards explain each of these parts, from single transistors to chips. By the end, you will know how active parts let a small signal control a larger current, and how they combine into the chips inside audio equipment.
