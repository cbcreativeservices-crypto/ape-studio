Topic Foundations: Electromechanical Parts and Hardware

Imagine turning the volume knob on a radio, pressing its power button, and watching a light come on. Each act depends on a small part inside the radio. What are these parts, and what does each one do?

Behind the knob is a strip of material that resists electricity. A small metal contact slides along the strip as you turn, and its position sets how much signal goes through. This part is called a potentiometer. The same part also comes as a slider, called a fader, which moves in a straight line. Our ears do not hear changes in loudness evenly, so a volume control changes the level only a little at first. The shape of that change is called the taper.

Many jobs only need on or off, or a choice between two paths. A switch does this with metal contacts that touch or separate. Contacts can bounce for a moment when they close, and they can spark when they open. Some knobs on digital equipment have no end point. They turn all the way around and send counted steps to a chip. Such a knob is called an encoder.

A switch needs a hand to move it. Sometimes the circuit must switch itself, for example to keep the speakers silent while an amplifier turns on. A small current turns a coil into a magnet, which moves the contacts. This part is called a relay.

Magnetism can also carry the signal itself. Two coils of wire are wound on a shared iron core, with no wire joining them. The changing signal in one coil creates the signal in the other. This part is called an audio transformer. Because the coils do not touch, it keeps two connected devices electrically separate, which can reduce hum. The number of wire loops on one coil compared with the other is called the turns ratio. It sets how much the voltage rises or falls.

Transformers also have a second job. A larger one lowers the voltage from the wall socket to what the equipment needs. Other parts then change it into a steady voltage that pushes current one way. Some equipment runs from batteries instead. If too much current flows, a fuse breaks the circuit. Power parts get hot, so metal plates called heat sinks carry heat away.

Along the signal path, the level often needs to be lowered or shown. A few resistors can lower the level by one fixed amount. This group is called an attenuator pad. A meter shows how high the level is. It may be a moving needle, a row of small lights, or a small screen.

The flashcards explain each part in detail, with its types and ratings. By the end, you will know what each part inside audio equipment does, and how it moves, switches, or uses magnetism.
