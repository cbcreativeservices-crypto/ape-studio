Topic Foundations: Equalization

Imagine a music player with a bass knob for low sounds and a treble knob for high sounds. Turn up the bass, and the low drum notes grow louder. Most sounds contain low, middle and high frequencies. A device that raises or lowers chosen frequencies is called an equalizer. So which frequencies can it change, by how much, and why?

An equalizer works on chosen parts of the frequency range, called bands, and each band has a shape. One shape, called a bell, changes a range around one center frequency. Another shape raises or lowers everything below a chosen frequency, and it is called a low shelf. The same change above a chosen frequency is called a high shelf. A filter strongly reduces everything above or below a chosen frequency.

How wide is each change? For a bell, a number called Q sets the width. A high Q changes a narrow range, and a low Q changes a wide range. For a filter, the slope tells how fast the level falls past its set frequency. A unit with frequency, amount and width controls on every band is called a parametric equalizer. A unit with many fixed bands, each with its own slider, is called a graphic equalizer.

Filters also differ in how they treat the sounds they keep. One design keeps the frequencies it lets through at the same level. Others are steeper, but the level of the frequencies they keep rises and falls slightly. Another keeps the frequencies in step, so sharp sounds do not ring or smear. Its slope is more gradual. Most equalizers shift some frequencies slightly later than others. One kind delays every frequency by the same amount. The cost is extra delay and sometimes a faint sound just before a sharp hit.

These changes are fixed, but some problems appear only sometimes, such as one loud note. An equalizer band can also act only when its frequencies become too loud or too quiet. That is called dynamic equalization.

Which change should you make? Often the goal is correction. Two instruments with shared frequencies can make each other hard to hear, so engineers often lower that range in one. Other changes are creative, such as more high frequencies on a voice.

Some equalizers measure the sound first. One kind changes a sound to match the frequency balance of another. Another reduces some changes a room makes to the sound of its speakers. That is called room correction. The room is often measured with a test sound called pink noise.
