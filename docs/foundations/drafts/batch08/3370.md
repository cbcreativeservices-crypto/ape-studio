Topic Foundations: Reverb

Imagine clapping once in an empty concert hall. The clap ends, but the sound stays and slowly fades. In a small booth with soft walls, the same clap stops almost at once. So how do you make a sound seem to be in a large room when it was recorded in a small one?

In a real space, sound bounces off the walls, floor and ceiling. A few separate echoes arrive first. Then many more echoes blend into one sound that fades. That fading part is called the reverb tail, and the whole effect is called reverb. The original sound with no effect is called dry. The sound of the effect is called wet.

Early studios created this effect with real objects. Some played the sound through a speaker in an empty room with hard walls and recorded the echoes. Others made a large metal sheet or long metal springs vibrate. Today most reverb is made by a computer. One method sends the sound through short delays whose output is sent back into their input, a little quieter each time. Another method uses a recording of how a real room responds to one short, sharp sound. Your recording then sounds as if it were in that room.

Every reverb has controls. A short pause between the original sound and the start of the reverb is called pre-delay. It keeps the start of each word clear. Other controls set how long the tail lasts and how large the room seems. In a real room, curtains and air reduce high frequencies faster than low ones, so the tail loses its high frequencies first. The control that copies this is called damping.

A computer reverb has one problem that real rooms do not have. Its delays never change, so the tail can keep sounding at a few fixed pitches. Many reverbs slowly change the delay times. They also make the left and right sides a little different, so the space seems to surround the listener.

In a song, engineers usually send several instruments to one shared reverb. Too much reverb makes the words hard to understand. So engineers often lower the reverb while the singer sings and raise it in the pauses. They also often remove its lowest and highest frequencies.

Other reverbs are designed to sound unnatural on purpose. A tail can stop suddenly after a drum hit, grow louder before a note instead of fading after it, or rise in pitch as it repeats.

The flashcards explain the older machines, the computer methods, the controls, and how reverb is used in a mix and as a special effect. By the end, you will know how artificial reverb is made and what each control changes. You will also know how reverb makes a sound seem to be in a room while it stays clear.
