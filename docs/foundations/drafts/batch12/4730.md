Topic Foundations: Advanced and Specialist Instruments

In the topic on test and measurement instruments, you learned about everyday meters and analyzers. But how do you know that a meter's number is correct? And what do you use when an ordinary microphone cannot measure what you need?

In that topic, each calibrator was checked against a better reference. Each reference is checked against a better one again, up to a national standards laboratory. This unbroken series is called the traceability chain. Each step adds a little doubt, so a trusted result states a range where the true value most likely is. That range is called the measurement uncertainty.

At the top of the chain, there is nothing better to compare with. But a good measurement microphone can also work as a very small loudspeaker. How well it sends sound is directly related to how well it receives it. So a laboratory tests several such microphones in pairs, one sending and one receiving. A calculation from the results gives each microphone's exact sensitivity. This method is called reciprocity calibration.

The result also depends on where the sound comes from. In a tiny sealed space, the pressure is the same everywhere. In open air, sound arrives from one direction. In a room with many reflections, it arrives from every direction. Corrections convert a reading between these situations. A room whose surfaces absorb almost all sound, except the lowest frequencies, is called a free-field chamber.

Microphones measure pressure, but pressure does not show which way sound energy moves. So a pair of matched microphones is held a small distance apart. The difference between their signals shows how the air moves, and together they give the amount and direction of the energy flow. That pair of microphones is called a sound-intensity probe. Its microphones must match closely in timing, or it shows a flow that is not there.

Much sound starts in a vibrating surface, such as a loudspeaker cone. A sensor attached to the part measures its movement. But on a very light part, the weight of a sensor changes the movement. A very hot part can also damage a sensor. So a laser is pointed at the surface, and the movement changes the frequency of the reflected light. That instrument is called a laser Doppler vibrometer.

To test a whole system, engineers send it a known signal. A pure tone that rises steadily in pitch lets processing separate the clean response from the distortion. Noise that seems random but repeats exactly lets an analyzer compare input with output accurately. Every analyzer adds a tiny distortion itself, which limits what it can measure.

The flashcards cover these methods, along with test rooms, robot scanners and electrical safety testers. By the end, you will know how a measurement is made trustworthy, and which instrument is chosen when everyday tools are not enough.
